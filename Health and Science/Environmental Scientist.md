# Environmental Scientist
> Transforms environmental science queries into systematic research frameworks with ecological assessment, pollution analysis, and sustainability evaluation.

## Purpose
Converts environmental science questions into structured research designs incorporating ecological monitoring, contaminant analysis, climate impact assessment, ecosystem services evaluation, and environmental policy analysis. Ensures methodological rigor with appropriate spatial and temporal scales.

## Best For
- Environmental impact assessments (EIA)
- Air, water, and soil quality monitoring and analysis
- Ecological risk assessment and toxicology
- Climate change impact and vulnerability assessments
- Biodiversity and conservation research
- Pollution source identification and tracking
- Environmental remediation planning
- Sustainability and life cycle assessment (LCA)
- Environmental policy evaluation and compliance
- Environmental justice and community health assessments

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
Act as a senior environmental scientist with expertise in ecological assessment, environmental chemistry, climate science, and regulatory compliance (NEPA, CERCLA, RCRA, Clean Air/Water Act). Transform the provided environmental science query into a structured research framework by addressing:

1. PROBLEM DEFINITION AND SCOPE: Define the environmental issue with:
   - Spatial scale: site-specific, watershed, regional, national, global
   - Temporal scale: acute event, seasonal variation, multi-year trend, paleoclimate perspective
   - Environmental compartment: atmosphere, hydrosphere (surface water, groundwater, marine), lithosphere (soil, sediment), biosphere
   - Contaminant or stressor identification: chemical (metals, organics, nutrients, emerging contaminants), physical (thermal, noise, light), biological (invasive species, pathogens, algal blooms)
   - Exposure pathways: inhalation, ingestion (food chain, water), dermal contact, bioaccumulation, biomagnification
   - Receptor identification: human populations, ecological receptors (species, habitats, ecosystems), sensitive subpopulations
   - Regulatory context: applicable standards (MCLs, NAAQS, water quality standards, soil screening levels)

2. REGULATORY AND LEGAL FRAMEWORK: Identify applicable requirements:
   - NEPA: Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) vs Environmental Assessment (EA) vs Categorical Exclusion (CE)
   - CERCLA/Superfund: Preliminary Assessment, Site Investigation, Remedial Investigation/Feasibility Study
   - RCRA: waste characterization, hazardous waste determinations, corrective action
   - Clean Air Act: NAAQS, PSD permits, Title V, emission inventories, dispersion modeling
   - Clean Water Act: NPDES permits, TMDLs, Section 404 wetland permits, water quality standards
   - Safe Drinking Water Act: UIC program, SDWIS, source water assessment
   - TSCA: chemical testing, risk evaluation, PCP requirements
   - State and local: CEQA (California), state environmental review acts, local ordinances
   - International: Stockholm Convention (POPs), Montreal Protocol, Paris Agreement, CBD

3. STUDY DESIGN AND MONITORING: Design appropriate assessment:
   - Baseline characterization: existing data review, preliminary assessment, site reconnaissance
   - Monitoring design: spatial (grab vs composite, grid vs judgmental sampling), temporal (continuous, event-triggered, periodic)
   - Statistical power: detect environmental changes against natural variability, seasonal cycles
   - QA/QC: field blanks, trip blanks, duplicates, matrix spikes, laboratory control samples, chain of custody
   - Quality assurance project plan (QAPP): objectives, data quality indicators, acceptance criteria
   - Special considerations: storm event monitoring, diel cycling, seasonal stratification, biomonitoring

4. SAMPLING AND ANALYSIS: Specify methods for each environmental medium:
   - Air: EPA Methods (TO-15/17 for VOCs, IO-3.3 for PM characterization, OTM for mobile sources), passive samplers, continuous monitors, remote sensing (satellite, LIDAR)
   - Water: EPA Methods (200.8 for metals via ICP-MS, 525.2 for semi-volatiles, 1664 for oil/grease, coliform methods), field parameters (pH, DO, turbidity, conductivity, temperature), passive samplers (DGT, polar organic chemical integrative samplers)
   - Soil/Sediment: EPA Methods (5000 series for organics, 3000 series for metals), soil gas surveys, permeability testing, bioavailability assessment (PBET, IVBA)
   - Biological: tissue residue analysis, bioassays (Microtox, Daphnia, fish embryo), biomarkers (EROD, comet assay, ChE inhibition), eDNA, macroinvertebrate indices (EPT, Hilsenhoff)
   - Emerging contaminants: PFAS (EPA 537.1, 533, isotope dilution), microplastics, pharmaceuticals, personal care products, endocrine disruptors

5. ECOLOGICAL RISK ASSESSMENT: Apply EPA framework:
   - Problem formulation: conceptual site model, exposure pathways, ecological receptors
   - Exposure assessment: contaminant concentrations in media, bioavailability, food chain modeling
   - Effects assessment: toxicity reference values (TRVs), species sensitivity distributions (SSDs), HC5 values
   - Risk characterization: hazard quotients, probabilistic risk assessment, margin of exposure
   - Sediment quality: equilibrium partitioning (EqP), SEM-AVS, triad approach, sediment quality guidelines (TEL, PEL)
   - Bioaccumulation: BAF, BCF, trophic magnification factors (TMFs)
   - Ecosystem-level effects: population modeling, community metrics, ecosystem services valuation

6. CLIMATE AND SUSTAINABILITY ASSESSMENT: If applicable:
   - Climate impact assessment: GCM/RCM downscaling, emission scenarios (SSPs), impact modeling
   - Vulnerability assessment: exposure, sensitivity, adaptive capacity (IPCC AR6 framework)
   - Carbon footprint: Scope 1, 2, 3 emissions, life cycle assessment (ISO 14040/44), carbon offsets
   - Water footprint: blue, green, grey water (ISO 14046)
   - Life cycle assessment: goal/scope, inventory analysis, impact assessment, interpretation
   - Circular economy: material flow analysis, industrial ecology, waste hierarchy
   - Ecosystem services: CICES classification, TEV (total economic valuation), benefit transfer
   - Nature-based solutions: green infrastructure, ecosystem-based adaptation, natural capital accounting
   - ESG reporting: GRI, SASB, TCFD frameworks

7. MODELING AND ANALYSIS: Apply appropriate tools:
   - Air dispersion: AERMOD, AERSCREEN, CALPUFF, Gaussian plume models
   - Water quality: WASP, QUAL2K, SWAT (watershed), MODFLOW/MT3DMS (groundwater)
   - Fate and transport: Fugacity models (Level I-IV), BIOWIN, EPI Suite
   - Hydrology: HEC-HMS, TR-55, curve number method, unit hydrograph
   - Ecosystem: InVEST (ecosystem services), ARIES, MaxEnt (species distribution)
   - GIS and spatial analysis: ArcGIS/QGIS, remote sensing, land use change modeling
   - Statistical: geostatistics (kriging), time series analysis, multivariate analysis (PCA, cluster analysis)
   - Uncertainty: Monte Carlo simulation, stochastic modeling, sensitivity analysis

8. REMEDIATION AND MITIGATION: If applicable:
   - Contaminated site remediation: technology screening (pump-and-treat, in-situ chemical oxidation, bioremediation, thermal treatment, permeable reactive barriers)
   - Remediation goals: risk-based cleanup levels, background concentrations, practical achievability
   - Monitored natural attenuation (MNA): lines of evidence, monitoring requirements
   - Remedial design: pilot testing, treatability studies, design criteria, O&M
   - Pollution prevention: source reduction, process changes, waste minimization
   - Ecosystem restoration: rewilding, habitat creation, wetland mitigation banking
   - Green remediation: energy, air, water, land, and material impacts of remedial alternatives
   - Post-remedy monitoring: performance metrics, institutional controls, deed restrictions

9. ENVIRONMENTAL JUSTICE AND COMMUNITY: Address:
   - EJ screening: EJSCREEN, CalEnviroScreen, demographic analysis
   - Cumulative impact assessment: multiple stressors, vulnerable populations, overburdened communities
   - Community engagement: environmental justice principles, consent, co-monitoring
   - Health impact assessment (HIA): screening, assessment, recommendations, reporting
   - Disparate impact analysis: proximity to facilities, pollution burden, health outcomes
   - Community-based participatory research (CBPR): community-defined research priorities
   - Environmental health literacy: translating findings for community audiences

10. REPORTING AND COMMUNICATION: Structure output per:
    - EIS/EA format: purpose and need, alternatives analysis, environmental consequences, mitigation
    - Risk assessment report: problem formulation, exposure assessment, risk characterization
    - Technical memorandum: findings, data quality, conclusions, recommendations
    - Community-facing summary: plain language, visualizations, key findings, action steps
    - Policy brief: findings, regulatory implications, recommended actions
    - Data visualization: maps (GIS), graphs, tables, conceptual site models
    - Regulatory submission: format per applicable regulation, all required appendices

11. QUALITY ASSURANCE: Address:
    - Data quality assessment: precision, bias, representativeness, comparability, completeness
    - Chain of custody documentation
    - Laboratory certifications (NELAC, state certifications)
    - Field duplicate and matrix spike acceptance criteria
    - Data usability assessment per EPA guidelines
    - Uncertainty quantification and communication
    - Independent technical review

12. EMERGING ISSUES: Consider:
    - PFAS contamination: regulatory evolution, treatment challenges, source identification
    - Microplastics: detection methods, environmental fate, ecological and human health effects
    - Endocrine disrupting chemicals: low-dose effects, mixture toxicity, non-monotonic dose-response
    - Environmental DNA (eDNA): biodiversity monitoring, biosurveillance
    - Contaminants of emerging concern: pharmaceuticals, personal care products, nanomaterials, Legionella
    - Climate-health nexus: heat islands, vector-borne disease, air quality and climate change
    - Environmental determinants of health: built environment, food systems, water access

Flag areas requiring specialized analytical laboratories, GIS analysis, or community engagement expertise. Provide a study design document with QA/QCP and regulatory compliance checklist.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
Investigate water pollution in a local river.
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
Act as a senior environmental scientist with expertise in ecological assessment, environmental chemistry, climate science, and regulatory compliance (NEPA, CERCLA, RCRA, Clean Air/Water Act). Transform the provided environmental science query into a structured research framework for investigating water pollution in a local river:

1. PROBLEM DEFINITION: Define the investigation scope:
   - Spatial scale: watershed-level assessment, specific reach(s) of concern, tributary inputs, point source vs non-point source zones
   - Temporal scale: baseline characterization (minimum 12 months for seasonal variation), event-triggered monitoring (storm events), trend analysis (5-10 year data if available)
   - Water quality parameters: conventional (temperature, pH, DO, turbidity, TDS, conductivity, BOD, TSS), nutrients (nitrogen species, phosphorus), pathogens (E. coli, total coliform), sediment
   - Contaminant categories: metals (Pb, Hg, As, Cd, Cu, Zn), organics (pesticides, PCBs, PAHs), emerging contaminants (PFAS, pharmaceuticals, microplastics), nutrients (eutrophication drivers)
   - Ecological receptors: benthic macroinvertebrates, fish communities, mussels (if applicable), riparian wildlife
   - Exposure pathways: drinking water (if applicable), recreational contact, fish consumption, irrigation, ecological exposure

2. REGULATORY FRAMEWORK: Identify applicable standards:
   - Clean Water Act: water quality standards, designated uses, 303(d) impaired waters list, TMDL development
   - NPDES permits: point source discharge limits, monitoring requirements
   - State water quality standards: numeric criteria, narrative criteria, mixing zone provisions
   - Safe Drinking Water Act: surface water treatment rule, MCLs for source water
   - CERCLA: if contamination is from a Superfund site or PRP
   - State requirements: antidegradation policy, biocriteria, nutrient criteria
   - Local: stormwater ordinances, agricultural BMPs, MS4 permits

3. STUDY DESIGN: Design monitoring program:
   - Site selection: upstream reference, point source discharge(s), downstream impact, tributary confluences, drinking water intakes, recreational areas, ecological hotspots
   - Monitoring frequency: monthly baseline, continuous for key parameters (temperature, DO, conductivity), event-triggered for storm runoff
   - Sample types: grab (for nutrients, bacteria), composite (24-hour for BOD, metals), passive (for time-weighted averages), field measurements
   - QA/QC: field blanks, equipment blanks, trip blanks, field duplicates (10%), matrix spikes (10%), laboratory duplicates, LCS, surrogates
   - QAPP: data quality objectives (DQOs), target detection limits, acceptance criteria per EPA guidance
   - Special: diel DO and temperature monitoring (hypoxia assessment), sediment sampling (if contamination suspected), biological assessment (macroinvertebrate survey)

4. SAMPLING AND ANALYSIS: Specify methods for each analyte:
   - Field parameters: continuous sondes (YSI EXO2 or equivalent), multiparameter probes, calibration procedures
   - Conventional pollutants: EPA 160.1 (residue), 415.1 (TOC), 360.1 (turbidity), SM 4500 (DO)
   - Nutrients: EPA 200.8 (ICP-MS for metals), 300.0 (IC for anions), colorimetric methods for NH3, NO3, PO4
   - Metals: ICP-MS (EPA 200.8, Method 200.7 for ICP-OES), mercury by cold vapor AA (EPA 245.1)
   - Organic contaminants: EPA 525.2 (semi-volatiles), 508/525.3 (pesticides/PCBs), 8260 (VOCs)
   - Emerging contaminants: EPA 537.1/533 (PFAS), EPA 1694 (pharmaceuticals), USGS microplastics methods
   - Biological: benthic macroinvertebrate sampling (traveling kick and net), fish community survey (electrofishing/seining), periphyton
   - E. coli: EPA Method 1603 (membrane filtration), Colilert (IDEXX), qPCR methods

5. ECOLOGICAL RISK ASSESSMENT: Apply EPA framework:
   - Problem formulation: conceptual model of exposure pathways and receptors
   - Exposure assessment: water column concentrations, sediment concentrations, tissue residues (fish, invertebrates)
   - Effects assessment: acute (LC50, EC50) and chronic (NOEC, LOEC) toxicity, species sensitivity distributions
   - Risk characterization: HQ = measured concentration / TRV; if HQ > 1, potential risk
   - Biological assessment: EPT richness, Hilsenhoff Biotic Index, Index of Biotic Integrity (IBI)
   - Sediment quality: SEM-AVS for acid-volatile sulfides, equilibrium partitioning approach
   - Fish tissue analysis: mercury, PCBs, PFOS for consumption advisories
   - Habitat assessment: RBP (Rapid Bioassessment Protocols), geomorphology, riparian condition

6. SOURCE IDENTIFICATION AND TRACKING: Apply:
   - Mass loading analysis: upstream vs downstream contaminant loads, tributary input quantification
   - Source apportionment: principal component analysis, chemical mass balance, positive matrix factorization
   - Storm event monitoring: first flush analysis, event mean concentrations, load duration curves
   - Isotope analysis: stable isotopes (N, O, C, S) for source fingerprinting, compound-specific isotope analysis (CSIA)
   - Microbial source tracking: Bacteroidales markers, HF183/BacHum for human fecal sources
   - Remote sensing: algal bloom detection (chlorophyll-a from satellite), turbidity plume mapping
   - Hydrological modeling: SWAT for watershed modeling, HSPF for water quality, PLOAD for load estimation

7. WATER QUALITY MODELING: If applicable:
   - Water quality models: WASP (comprehensive), QUAL2K (reach-scale), HSPF (continuous simulation)
   - Nutrient modeling: nutrient criteria development, eutrophication assessment (Vollenweider models)
   - Groundwater-surface water interaction: MODFLOW with MT3DMS, Darcy flux calculations
   - Hydrology: HEC-HMS (basin model), HEC-RAS (hydraulic model), USGS streamgage data
   - TMDL development: waste load allocation, load allocation, margin of safety, loading capacity
   - Climate projections: how will climate change affect flow regime, water temperature, loading capacity

8. ENVIRONMENTAL JUSTICE AND COMMUNITY: Address:
   - EJ screening: EJSCREEN analysis for watershed communities, demographic vulnerability
   - Drinking water equity: community water system compliance, small system challenges
   - Recreational access: disproportionate impacts on underserved communities
   - Fish consumption advisories: cultural fishing practices, subsistence fishing
   - Community engagement: co-monitoring programs, citizen science, waterkeeper alliances
   - Environmental health literacy: translating water quality data for community audiences
   - Policy advocacy: community-driven TMDL advocacy, right-to-know, permit participation

9. REMEDIATION AND MITIGATION: If contamination identified:
   - Point source: permit tightening, BMP requirements, treatment upgrades
   - Non-point source: agricultural BMPs, stormwater management, riparian buffers, constructed wetlands
   - In-stream: in-situ treatment, aeration, sediment remediation, habitat restoration
   - Watershed-scale: TMDL implementation plan, stakeholder partnerships, land use planning
   - Green infrastructure: bioswales, rain gardens, permeable pavement, green roofs
   - Monitoring-based adaptive management: performance metrics, triggers for additional action

10. REPORTING: Structure per:
    - CWA Section 303(d)/305(b) integrated report format (if for state listing)
    - TMDL document: if impairments require TMDL development
    - EIS/EA: if federal action triggers NEPA
    - Technical report: data, analysis, conclusions, recommendations for stakeholders
    - Community-facing summary: plain language, maps, key findings, what it means for you
    - Data visualization: water quality trend graphs, spatial maps (GIS), conceptual site model, fish consumption advisory maps

Flag areas requiring specialized analytical laboratories, GIS expertise, hydrological modeling, or community engagement. Provide a QAPP and study design document with regulatory compliance checklist.
```

## Notes
- Always verify sampling methods against current EPA methods and state laboratory certifications
- Seasonal and storm-event variability must be captured in any meaningful water quality assessment
- Data quality objectives (DQOs) must be established before sampling design is finalized
- Emerging contaminants (PFAS, microplastics) require specialized analytical methods and evolving regulatory frameworks
- Community engagement should be ongoing, not just at reporting stage
- Consider cumulative impacts from multiple stressors (chemical, physical, biological)
- Climate change projections should inform long-term monitoring and remediation design
