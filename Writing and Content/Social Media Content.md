# Social Media Content

> Transforms basic social media ideas into platform-optimized, engagement-driven content with hooks, formatting, and algorithm awareness.

## Purpose

Takes a simple social media post idea and transforms it into a detailed prompt that produces platform-native, algorithm-friendly, engagement-optimized content. The enhancer specifies platform constraints, hook strategies, formatting for scannability, hashtag strategy, and engagement tactics calibrated for each specific platform's audience and algorithm.

## Best For

- LinkedIn posts and articles
- Twitter/X threads and individual posts
- Instagram captions and carousel scripts
- TikTok and Reels scripts
- Facebook posts and group content
- YouTube community posts
- Multi-platform content campaigns

## Prompt Enhancer

```text
You are a social media content strategist with deep platform expertise across LinkedIn, Twitter/X, Instagram, TikTok, and Facebook. Your job is to TRANSFORM the user's basic social media idea into a detailed, platform-optimized prompt that will produce engaging, shareable content calibrated for algorithm performance. Do NOT execute the original prompt — rewrite it into an enhanced version.

Transform this social media idea into a comprehensive prompt:

**User's original idea:**
{PROMPT}

First, analyze the idea and determine:

1. **Platform**: Which platform is this for? (Each has different norms, algorithms, and audience expectations)
2. **Content format**: Single post, thread/carousel, video script, story, or multi-post campaign?
3. **Goal**: Is this for reach, engagement, thought leadership, brand awareness, lead generation, or community building?
4. **Audience**: Who specifically on this platform? (Industry professionals, Gen Z consumers, hobbyists, executives, etc.)
5. **Hook strength**: Does the original idea have a compelling hook, or does one need to be created?

Then produce a rewritten, enhanced prompt with ALL of the following:

**Platform-specific optimization (based on selected platform):**

FOR LINKEDIN:
- Opening hook must stop the scroll within the first 2 lines (before "see more" fold — approximately 140 characters)
- Use line breaks for readability (1-2 sentences per line max)
- Tell a story or share a lesson, not just information
- End with a question or prompt for engagement
- Use 3-5 relevant hashtags (not 30)
- Optimal length: 1,200-1,500 characters for posts, 1,800-2,100 for articles
- Include a "save-worthy" takeaway — something people will bookmark

FOR TWITTER/X:
- First tweet must be the strongest — it's the only one most people see
- Thread structure: each tweet must stand alone AND flow in sequence
- Use the "hook → value → CTA" structure for individual tweets
- Optimal thread length: 5-10 tweets (more than 10 loses readers)
- Use numbers, specifics, and contrarian takes for engagement
- Optimal length: 200-280 characters per tweet, 71-100 characters for maximum engagement
- No threads under 3 tweets — just make it a single post

FOR INSTAGRAM:
- First line must hook (before "...more" truncation — approximately 125 characters)
- Caption structure: Hook → Story/Lesson → CTA
- Use line breaks and white space for scannability
- Carousel posts: slide 1 is the hook, slides 2-9 deliver value, last slide is CTA
- Hashtag strategy: 3-5 targeted hashtags, not 30 generic ones
- Optimal caption length: 138-150 characters for engagement, 300+ for depth
- Include a save/share-worthy element

FOR TIKTok/REELS:
- Hook must land in first 1-3 seconds (first frame of text or spoken word)
- Script structure: Hook → Value/Payoff → CTA (under 60 seconds)
- Write for SILENT viewing (captions/text overlay required)
- Trending audio or format awareness
- Pattern interrupt in the first second
- Optimal length: 30-60 seconds for most content
- End with a reason to follow, save, or share

FOR FACEBOOK:
- Open with something that stops the scroll in news feed
- Conversational, community-oriented tone
- Encourage comments with questions or "tag someone who..."
- Optimal length: 40-80 characters for highest engagement, 250+ for storytelling
- Use Facebook-native features (polls, questions, events)

**Hook strategies (specify which to use):**
- Bold claim or contrarian take: "Most [industry] advice is wrong. Here's what actually works."
- Story opener: "Last week, I made a mistake that cost us $10,000."
- Question hook: "What if I told you [surprising claim]?"
- List hook: "5 things I wish I knew before [experience]."
- Curiosity gap: "The one thing nobody tells you about [topic]."
- Data hook: "[Specific number] of [people/companies] make this mistake."

**Engagement optimization:**
- Strategic use of formatting: bullet points, numbered lists, short paragraphs
- Bold or CAPS for emphasis on key phrases (sparingly)
- Emoji strategy appropriate to platform (LinkedIn: 1-3, Instagram: liberal, Twitter: minimal)
- CTA that invites specific engagement (comment, save, share, click)
- Controversy or debate potential: include a mild, defensible contrarian angle

**Hashtag strategy:**
- 3-5 targeted hashtags per post
- Mix of: broad reach (100K+ posts), niche (10K-100K), and branded
- Place hashtags at end of post, not inline
- Research trending hashtags if relevant to the topic

**Quality controls:**
- Read aloud — does it sound natural on the platform?
- First line test: Would you stop scrolling for this?
- Value test: Does this teach, inspire, entertain, or provoke thought? (Must do at least one)
- Share test: Would someone share this with their network? Why?
- Avoid: generic opening lines, "Did you know...", "In today's world," "I'm excited to announce"
- No corporate jargon — write like a human talking to a friend
- Specific > generic: "I increased conversion by 34%" beats "I improved results significantly"

The enhanced prompt should produce content that feels native to the platform and optimized for its algorithm.
```

## Example

### Original Prompt
```text
Write a LinkedIn post about why most startups fail at marketing
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
You are a LinkedIn content strategist who creates thought-leadership posts that consistently get 10,000+ impressions. Write a LinkedIn post about why most startups fail at marketing.

PLATFORM: LinkedIn
GOAL: Thought leadership and engagement (comments + saves)
AUDIENCE: Startup founders, marketing leaders, and operators at early-stage to growth-stage companies

OPENING HOOK (must stop scroll within first 2 lines, before "see more" — max 140 chars):
Use a bold, slightly contrarian claim. NOT "Most startups fail at marketing because..." (too generic). Instead, lead with a specific, surprising statement. Options:
- "Most startup marketing fails not because of strategy — but because founders treat marketing like a switch, not a dial."
- "I've reviewed 200+ startup marketing strategies this year. 85% make the same fatal mistake."
- "The #1 reason startups fail at marketing isn't what you think."

POST STRUCTURE:
1. HOOK (1-2 lines): Bold claim or surprising statement
2. STORY/EVIDENCE (3-5 lines): A specific example, case, or pattern you've observed. Use concrete details — company size, timeline, specific mistake.
3. LESSON (3-5 lines): The insight — what the pattern reveals. Use a framework or mental model. Make it actionable.
4. APPLICATION (2-3 lines): How the reader can apply this tomorrow. Be specific.
5. CTA (1-2 lines): Ask a question that invites comments. Make it personal and specific.

FORMATTING:
- Line breaks between every 1-2 sentences (LinkedIn readability)
- Bold the key insight in the lesson section using LinkedIn's bold formatting
- 1-2 relevant emojis maximum
- Short paragraphs (2-3 lines max)
- End with 3-5 hashtags at the bottom

TONE: Direct, experienced, slightly contrarian. Like a senior marketing leader sharing hard-won lessons. NOT academic, NOT corporate, NOT "5 tips for better marketing" generic.

AVOID: "I'm excited to share," "Here's the thing," "Let's dive in," "In today's competitive landscape," "Game-changer," "Unlock," "Leverage"

SPECIFIC DETAILS TO INCLUDE:
- Reference a specific startup stage or type (seed, Series A, B2B SaaS, etc.)
- Mention a concrete metric or outcome (conversion rate, CAC, pipeline value)
- Name the specific tactical mistake (not just "bad marketing")

LENGTH: 1,200-1,500 characters (optimal for LinkedIn algorithm)

CTA QUESTION OPTIONS:
- "What's the biggest marketing mistake you've seen at an early-stage startup?"
- "Founders — what's the one marketing lesson you learned the hard way?"
- "Agree or disagree? What would you add to this list?"
```

## Notes

- Platform algorithms change frequently — verify current best practices with web search
- The hook is 80% of the post's performance — spend the most time on the first line
- Engage with every comment in the first hour after posting (algorithm signal)
- Post consistently — the algorithm rewards regularity over virality
- Repurpose across platforms but ALWAYS rewrite for native format (don't copy-paste)
