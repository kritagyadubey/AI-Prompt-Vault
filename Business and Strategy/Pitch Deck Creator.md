# Pitch Deck Creator
> Transforms startup narratives into structured, compelling investor pitch decks with clear flow and data-backed claims.

## Purpose
This enhancer takes a founder's raw business description and transforms it into a complete, sequenced pitch deck outline with slide-by-slide content guidance, narrative arc, data points, and visual direction cues. It ensures every slide serves a purpose, the story builds logically, and the ask is clear and defensible. It prevents common mistakes like too much text, missing financials, or weak problem statements.

## Best For
- Founders preparing for investor meetings, demo days, or accelerator applications
- Teams creating pitch decks for venture capital, angel investors, or strategic partners
- Advisors helping startups polish their fundraising materials
- Entrepreneurs applying to pitch competitions
- Anyone needing to present a business opportunity in a structured, visual format

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
Act as a pitch deck expert and storytelling strategist who has reviewed thousands of investor presentations. Use web search to research current investor preferences, pitch deck best practices, and comparable company data to strengthen claims. Transform the user's business description into a complete, compelling pitch deck structure.

For each slide, provide: the slide title, the key message (one sentence), the content to include (specific data points, visuals, or text), speaker notes with talking points, and common mistakes to avoid on that slide.

Follow this pitch deck structure (12-15 slides):

1. COVER SLIDE
   - Company name, logo placeholder, one-line tagline that communicates value
   - Contact information
   - Slide design notes: clean, professional, minimal text

2. PROBLEM SLIDE
   - The problem stated in 1-2 sentences that create emotional resonance
   - Supporting data: market size of the problem, how many people suffer from it, cost of the problem (web search for relevant statistics)
   - A relatable scenario or story that makes the problem tangible
   - Visual: consider a before/after comparison or a pain-point illustration

3. SOLUTION SLIDE
   - Your solution in one clear sentence
   - 3-4 key features or capabilities that directly address the problem
   - How it works: a simple 3-step or before/after visual
   - Key differentiator: what makes this different from existing solutions?

4. MARKET OPPORTUNITY SLIDE
   - TAM, SAM, SOM with clear methodology (top-down AND bottom-up sizing)
   - Market growth rate and drivers (cite sources from web search)
   - Why now? What market shift or technology change makes this possible today?
   - Comparable company outcomes to demonstrate market potential

5. PRODUCT SLIDE
   - Screenshots or mockups of the actual product
   - Key features mapped to customer pain points
   - Technology moat or proprietary advantage
   - Product roadmap: current state and next 12 months
   - User metrics if available (retention, engagement, NPS)

6. TRACTION SLIDE
   - Key metrics: revenue, users, growth rate, retention, NPS
   - Growth trajectory chart (month-over-month or quarter-over-quarter)
   - Notable milestones, partnerships, or press
   - Comparison to benchmarks at similar stage (web search for typical metrics by stage)

7. BUSINESS MODEL SLIDE
   - Revenue streams and pricing
   - Unit economics: CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC, payback period, gross margin
   - How the business scales: what improves as you grow?
   - Path to profitability timeline

8. COMPETITION SLIDE
   - 2x2 positioning matrix (your axes should highlight your advantage)
   - Key competitors with honest assessment of strengths and weaknesses
   - Your sustainable competitive advantage or moat
   - Why customers choose you over alternatives

9. GO-TO-MARKET SLIDE
   - Customer acquisition strategy with channels and expected CAC
   - Sales motion: self-serve, inside sales, enterprise, or hybrid
   - Partnership and distribution strategy
   - Marketing channels with budget allocation
   - Timeline to reach key distribution milestones

10. TEAM SLIDE
    - Founders with relevant experience and achievements
    - Key hires already made or committed
    - Advisory board with notable names
    - Why this team is uniquely positioned to win

11. FINANCIALS SLIDE
    - 3-5 year projections: revenue, costs, EBITDA
    - Key assumptions explicitly stated
    - Revenue growth rate and path to profitability
    - Capital efficiency metrics

12. THE ASK SLIDE
    - Specific funding amount being raised
    - Use of funds with percentage allocation
    - Key milestones the funding will achieve
    - Timeline to next funding round or profitability
    - What investors get: terms, structure, timeline

13. VISION SLIDE (optional, for longer decks)
    - 3-5 year vision: what does the world look like when you succeed?
    - Ambitious but credible long-term goal
    - Emotional close that inspires action

14. APPENDIX SLIDES (if needed)
    - Detailed financials
    - Technical architecture
    - Customer testimonials or case studies
    - Press coverage

For each slide, also note: what NOT to include (common mistakes), how long to spend on it during a 10-minute presentation, and what question the slide should make investors ask.

Conclude with overall deck advice: narrative flow, design principles, and rehearsal tips.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
Create a pitch deck outline for my SaaS project management tool.
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
Act as a pitch deck expert who has reviewed thousands of investor presentations. Use web search to research current SaaS pitch deck benchmarks, typical metrics investors expect at seed/Series A stage, competitor landscape (Asana, Monday.com, Linear, Notion, ClickUp), and what VCs specifically look for in project management tools. Transform the description of a SaaS project management tool into a complete, compelling 12-slide pitch deck.

For each slide, provide the slide title, one-sentence key message, specific content including data points, speaker notes, and common mistakes to avoid.

Slide 2 (Problem): Research and cite specific data on project management failures — what percentage of projects run over budget, over time, or fail entirely? What does this cost businesses annually? What are the top complaints about existing tools (web search for G2/Capterra reviews)?

[Continue through all slides with project-management-SaaS-specific detail, current market data, and investor-relevant metrics.]
```

## Notes
- The enhancer builds a narrative arc, not just a list of slides — the story must build momentum toward the ask
- Use web search to find current investor preferences and comparable company data rather than generic advice
- Every data claim should be either sourced or flagged as an estimate that needs verification
- The enhancer prevents the most common pitch deck failure: too much text, not enough story
- Encourage founders to rehearse with the speaker notes, not just read the slides
