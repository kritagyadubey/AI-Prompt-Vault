# Fundraising Advisor
> Transforms fundraising goals into structured capital strategies with investor targeting, deck optimization, and negotiation frameworks.

## Purpose
This enhancer takes a startup's fundraising needs and transforms them into a comprehensive capital strategy. It covers investor identification and targeting, pitch refinement, data room preparation, term sheet analysis, and post-fundraise relationship management. It ensures founders approach fundraising as a strategic process with clear milestones, rather than a hopeful scatter-shot effort.

## Best For
- Founders preparing for pre-seed, seed, Series A, or later-stage rounds
- CFOs or finance leads building fundraising strategies
- Startups that need to understand valuation, dilution, and term implications
- Founders exploring alternative funding mechanisms (revenue-based financing, grants, loans)
- Advisors helping startups prepare for fundraising
- Any team that needs to build investor relationships before raising

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
Act as a fundraising strategist and investor relations expert with deep experience across venture capital, angel investing, and alternative funding mechanisms. Use web search to research current fundraising benchmarks (round sizes, valuations, dilution expectations), active investors in the user's sector and stage, recent comparable raises, and market sentiment. Transform the user's fundraising needs into a comprehensive capital strategy.

Work through these sections systematically:

1. FUNDRAISING READINESS ASSESSMENT
   - Current stage: pre-revenue, early revenue, scaling, growth
   - Business metrics: MRR/ARR, growth rate, burn rate, runway, unit economics
   - What stage-appropriate metrics do investors expect? (web search for benchmarks)
   - Readiness scorecard: what is strong, what needs work, what is missing?
   - Critical gaps to address before fundraising (metrics, team, product, traction)

2. CAPITAL STRATEGY DESIGN
   - How much to raise: calculation based on runway needs, growth targets, and milestone-based budgeting
   - Funding mechanism: equity, convertible note, SAFE, revenue-based financing, grants, debt
   - For each mechanism considered: pros, cons, dilution implications, investor expectations
   - Recommended structure with rationale
   - Milestone-based budget: what will the capital fund, and what metrics will it achieve?

3. INVESTOR TARGETING AND PIPELINE
   - Use web search to identify and categorize potential investors:
     - Tier 1: Best-fit investors (sector expertise, stage focus, check size match, portfolio synergy)
     - Tier 2: Good-fit investors (generalist, reasonable alignment)
     - Tier 3: Backup investors (less ideal fit but available capital)
   - For each tier, identify 10-15 specific investors with rationale
   - Investor research: recent investments, thesis alignment, decision-making style
   - Outreach strategy: warm introductions, cold outreach, events, platforms
   - Pipeline management: how many conversations to start, how many convert

4. PITCH REFINEMENT
   - Elevator pitch: 30-second version that creates curiosity
   - Core narrative: the story that makes investors care (problem → solution → market → traction → team → ask)
   - Key data points to emphasize: what metrics tell the strongest story?
   - Anticipated objections: top 5 investor concerns and pre-emptive responses
   - Demo and product narrative: what to show and how to frame it

5. DATA ROOM PREPARATION
   - Financial model: revenue projections, unit economics, sensitivity analysis
   - Cap table: current structure, option pool, post-money implications
   - Legal documents: incorporation, IP assignment, key contracts, board consents
   - Due diligence checklist: what investors will ask for and how to organize it
   - Data room organization: folder structure, naming conventions, access controls
   - Common data room red flags and how to address them proactively

6. VALUATION AND TERM SHEET ANALYSIS
   - Valuation methods: comparable transactions, revenue multiples, DCF, market approach
   - What valuation is realistic for this stage and metrics? (web search for comparable raises)
   - Dilution analysis: what ownership percentage will founders retain through the round?
   - Key term sheet terms explained:
     - Valuation (pre-money, post-money)
     - Liquidation preferences (1x non-participating vs. participating)
     - Anti-dilution provisions (broad-based vs. full ratchet)
     - Board composition and governance
     - Protective provisions
     - Pro rata rights
     - Information rights
     - Vesting and acceleration
   - Negotiation strategy: what to fight for, what to concede, what to walk away from
   - Red flags in term sheets and how to address them

7. FUNDRAISING PROCESS AND TIMELINE
   - Phase 1: Preparation (weeks 1-4) — metrics, data room, pitch refinement
   - Phase 2: Outreach (weeks 5-8) — investor meetings, relationship building
   - Phase 3: Negotiation (weeks 9-12) — term sheet, due diligence, closing
   - Phase 4: Post-close (weeks 13-16) — wire transfer, governance setup, investor onboarding
   - Meeting cadence and management: how to run an efficient process
   - Competitive tension: how to create urgency without burning bridges
   - Backup plan: what to do if the round doesn't come together on timeline

8. INVESTOR RELATIONSHIP MANAGEMENT
   - Pre-fundraise: how to build relationships before you need money
   - During fundraise: how to keep investors engaged and updated
   - Post-fundraise: board management, reporting cadence, expectations setting
   - Investor updates: monthly/quarterly format and content
   - When things go wrong: how to communicate bad news
   - Long-term relationship strategy: turning investors into advocates

9. ALTERNATIVE FUNDRAISING MECHANISMS
   - Bootstrapping strategies and milestones that improve fundraising position
   - Revenue-based financing: when it makes sense, providers, terms
   - Government grants and incentives: R&D credits, innovation grants
   - Strategic corporate investment: pros, cons, and how to approach
   - Crowdfunding: equity crowdfunding and rewards-based
   - When to pursue each mechanism vs. traditional VC

Present the output as a complete fundraising playbook with actionable steps, specific investor recommendations (via web search), valuation guidance, and a clear timeline. Include a fundraising readiness checklist and a term sheet comparison template.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
I need to raise a seed round for my AI startup. How should I approach it?
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
Act as a fundraising strategist. Use web search to research current AI startup seed round benchmarks (typical round sizes, valuations, dilution), active AI-focused seed investors, recent comparable AI startup raises, and current investor sentiment toward AI companies. Design a comprehensive fundraising strategy for an AI startup seed round.

1. FUNDRAISING READINESS ASSESSMENT
   - What metrics do seed-stage AI investors expect? (web search for benchmarks)
   - What is the current state: MVP, early traction, revenue, users?
   - What is the burn rate and runway?
   - What are the typical dilution expectations at seed stage?

[Continue through all 9 sections with AI-startup-specific fundraising strategy, investor targeting with names and rationale, and detailed timeline.]
```

## Notes
- Web search is critical for current investor landscape, comparable raises, and market sentiment
- The enhancer forces founders to approach fundraising as a process, not an event
- Valuation and term sheet analysis prevents founders from leaving money on the table or accepting harmful terms
- The post-fundraise section is often overlooked — investor relationships are long-term
- Alternative funding mechanisms should be considered, not just defaulting to VC
