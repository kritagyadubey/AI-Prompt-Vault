# Lesson Plan Creator
> Transforms a topic and timeframe into a detailed, standards-aligned lesson plan with timing, activities, differentiation, and assessment.

## Purpose
This enhancer converts a topic name and class duration into a complete, teachable lesson plan. It prevents the AI from producing vague, activity-heavy plans without pedagogical purpose by enforcing alignment between objectives, activities, and assessment, and by requiring specific timing, materials, and differentiation strategies.

## Best For
- Student teachers creating lesson plans for practicum placements
- Experienced teachers preparing new units
- Homeschooling parents structuring daily lessons
- Corporate trainers designing workshop sessions
- Anyone who needs to teach something and wants a structured plan

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
You are an expert lesson plan architect trained in the Madeline Hunter model, Understanding by Design (UbD), Gradual Release of Responsibility (I Do, We Do, You Do), and differentiated instruction (Tomlinson). Transform the following topic into a complete, production-ready lesson plan.

ANALYZE THE INPUT:
- What is the subject and specific topic?
- What grade level / age group / learner population?
- How long is the lesson? (minutes)
- What is the broader unit context? (Where does this lesson fit in a sequence?)
- What prior knowledge can you assume?
- Are there any specific constraints? (resources, class size, ability range, standards)

PRODUCE THE FOLLOWING STRUCTURED LESSON PLAN:

1. LESSON HEADER
   - Lesson title (descriptive, engaging)
   - Subject and grade level
   - Duration (exact minutes)
   - Date placeholder and unit context

2. LEARNING OBJECTIVES
   - Write 2-4 objectives using measurable verbs (Bloom's Taxonomy)
   - Include at least one objective at each cognitive level present in the lesson
   - Make objectives student-facing ("Students will be able to...")
   - Ensure objectives are specific and assessable within the lesson timeframe

3. STANDARDS ALIGNMENT (if applicable)
   - Map each objective to relevant curriculum standards
   - Include standard codes and brief descriptions
   - Note if the lesson addresses prerequisite standards

4. MATERIALS AND RESOURCES
   - List all physical materials needed (with quantities)
   - List all digital resources with URLs or platform references
   - List all handouts/worksheets (describe content, not just name)
   - Note preparation time required and any advance setup

5. LESSON SEQUENCE (Minute-by-Minute)
   Structure the lesson using the Gradual Release model:

   a. HOOK / ENGAGE (5-10% of time)
      - Specific activity to capture attention and activate prior knowledge
      - Connection to student interests or real-world relevance
      - Concrete question, scenario, or provocation

   b. I DO — DIRECT INSTRUCTION (20-30% of time)
      - What the teacher models, demonstrates, or explains
      - Key vocabulary to introduce (with student-friendly definitions)
      - Visual aids, diagrams, or anchor charts to reference
      - Check for understanding moment (specific questions to ask)

   c. WE DO — GUIDED PRACTICE (25-35% of time)
      - Structured activity where students practice with teacher support
      - Grouping strategy (pairs, small groups, whole class)
      - Specific scaffolding questions to guide students
      - Common errors to watch for and how to address them in real-time
      - Transition phrase to move to independent work

   d. YOU DO — INDEPENDENT PRACTICE (25-35% of time)
      - Individual or pair activity demonstrating mastery
      - Differentiation options (extension for advanced, scaffolds for struggling)
      - Success criteria students should use to self-assess
      - Circulation plan — where the teacher moves and who to check on first

   e. CLOSURE / WRAP-UP (5-10% of time)
      - Specific closure activity (not just "review what we learned")
      - Exit ticket or formative assessment with specific questions
      - Connection preview to next lesson
      - Student reflection prompt

6. DIFFERENTIATION STRATEGIES
   - For English Language Learners: specific language supports
   - For students with IEPs/504s: specific accommodations
   - For advanced learners: extension activities or enrichment
   - For students who finish early: meaningful follow-up tasks
   - For students who struggle: reteaching approach during independent practice

7. ASSESSMENT PLAN
   - Formative assessments embedded in the lesson (with specific questions/tasks)
   - Criteria for determining if the lesson objective was met
   - What data to collect and how to use it for next lesson planning
   - Specific rubric or scoring guide for the exit ticket

8. TEACHER REFLECTION PROMPTS (Post-Lesson)
   - What went well? What evidence supports this?
   - What would I change? Why?
   - Which students need reteaching? What specifically do they need?
   - How should this inform tomorrow's lesson?

FORMAT the lesson plan so it can be printed and used immediately in a classroom. Use clear timing markers, bold key actions, and include all questions/launches verbatim (what to say, not just what to do).
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
Make a lesson plan for teaching photosynthesis to 9th graders in 50 minutes.
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
You are an expert lesson plan architect trained in the Madeline Hunter model, Understanding by Design (UbD), Gradual Release of Responsibility (I Do, We Do, You Do), and differentiated instruction (Tomlinson). Transform the following topic into a complete, production-ready lesson plan.

TOPIC: Photosynthesis for 9th grade biology. 50-minute class period. This is the first lesson in a 3-lesson unit on energy in ecosystems. Students have basic cell biology knowledge. Class of 28 students with mixed abilities including 3 IEP students and 2 ELL students.

PRODUCE THE FOLLOWING STRUCTURED LESSON PLAN:

1. LESSON HEADER
2. LEARNING OBJECTIVES
3. STANDARDS ALIGNMENT
4. MATERIALS AND RESOURCES
5. LESSON SEQUENCE (Minute-by-Minute)
6. DIFFERENTIATION STRATEGIES
7. ASSESSMENT PLAN
8. TEACHER REFLECTION PROMPTS

FORMAT the lesson plan so it can be printed and used immediately in a classroom. Use clear timing markers, bold key actions, and include all questions/launches verbatim.
```

## Notes
- The minute-by-minute sequencing prevents the common AI mistake of listing activities without timing.
- The Gradual Release model (I Do, We Do, You Do) ensures cognitive load is managed properly.
- Differentiation strategies are not optional add-ons — they're integrated into each phase.
- The post-lesson reflection prompts create a feedback loop for continuous improvement.
- The enhancer works for any subject, grade level, or duration — the framework adapts.
