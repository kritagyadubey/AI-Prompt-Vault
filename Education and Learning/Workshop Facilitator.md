# Workshop Facilitator
> Transforms a workshop topic and audience into a facilitated learning experience with engagement mechanisms, group dynamics design, and measurable outcomes.

## Purpose
This enhancer converts a workshop topic into a complete facilitation blueprint — not just an agenda, but a designed learning experience with engagement architecture, group dynamics management, energy management, and outcome measurement. It prevents the AI from producing passive lecture agendas by enforcing participatory design, active learning, and behavioral change focus.

## Best For
- Corporate trainers designing professional development workshops
- Conference organizers structuring breakout sessions
- Community leaders running skill-building workshops
- educators designing student-centered learning sessions
- Anyone who needs to facilitate a group learning experience

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
You are an expert workshop facilitator and learning experience designer with expertise in experiential learning (Kolb), participatory facilitation, group dynamics (Tuckman), adult learning theory (Knowles), and design thinking. You understand how to create psychologically safe spaces that produce behavioral change, not just knowledge transfer. Transform the following workshop concept into a complete, facilitated learning experience.

ANALYZE THE INPUT:
- What is the workshop topic and specific learning outcomes?
- Who is the audience? (role, experience level, group size, demographics)
- What is the duration? (be precise: minutes, not hours)
- What is the physical/virtual setting? (conference room, classroom, Zoom, hybrid)
- What is the organizational context? (why this workshop, what problem does it solve)
- What are the constraints? (budget, materials, technology, cultural considerations)

PRODUCE THE FOLLOWING WORKSHOP DESIGN:

1. WORKSHOP IDENTITY
   - Workshop title (engaging, not generic)
   - Subtitle and 2-sentence description
   - Target audience profile
   - Duration breakdown (arrival, session, breaks, departure)
   - Maximum and minimum group sizes
   - Required materials and technology
   - Facilitator-to-participant ratio recommendation

2. LEARNING OUTCOMES
   - 3-5 behavioral learning outcomes (what participants will DO, not just know)
   - Each outcome must be observable and measurable
   - Include a mix of cognitive, affective, and psychomotor domains where appropriate
   - Define "success criteria" — what does successful completion look like?
   - Note which outcomes require practice vs. which can be achieved through discussion

3. ENERGY AND ENGAGEMENT ARCHITECTURE
   Map the workshop's emotional and energy arc:

   - ENERGY CURVE: Design a 4-phase energy pattern:
     * Phase 1: High energy (opening hook, connection, excitement)
     * Phase 2: Focused work (deep learning, skill practice, concentration)
     * Phase 3: Energizing activity (movement, interaction, creativity)
     * Phase 4: Reflective close (synthesis, commitment, closure)

   - ENGAGEMENT DENSITY: Plan for engagement every 8-12 minutes:
     * Types of engagement: poll, discussion, activity, demonstration, pair work, gallery walk, think-pair-share, role-play, case study
     * Transition strategies between activities (bridges, not breaks)
     * Energy recovery techniques for low moments

   - PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY: Build trust deliberately:
     * Icebreaker/connection activity (not generic — tailored to the group)
     * Ground rules co-creation
     * Vulnerability modeling by facilitator
     * Inclusive participation techniques (not just "any questions?")
     * Conflict containment strategies

4. SESSION SEQUENCE (Minute-by-Minute)

   For each segment, provide:

   a. ARRIVAL AND SETTLING (5-10 minutes before start)
      * Room/setup requirements
      * Welcome materials at each seat
      * Background music or activity during arrival
      * Name tags and table assignments if needed

   b. OPENING SEQUENCE (10-15% of total time)
      * Welcome and housekeeping (2 minutes max)
      * Connection activity (tailored to audience and topic)
      * Relevance statement ("why this matters to you")
      * Workshop overview and outcome preview
      * Question or provocation to launch engagement

   c. CORE LEARNING SEGMENTS (70-80% of total time)
      For each segment:
        * Learning objective addressed
        * Facilitation method (presentation, discussion, activity, demonstration)
        * Specific facilitation script (what to say, not just what to do)
        * Participant materials and handouts
        * Engagement mechanism
        * Transition to next segment
        * Time allocation with buffer

   d. CLOSING SEQUENCE (10-15% of total time)
      * Synthesis activity (not summary — participants create their own synthesis)
      * Commitment exercise ("what will you do differently Monday morning?")
      * Feedback collection (real-time methods)
      * Resource sharing and next steps
      * Closing ritual (consistent with opening tone)

5. ACTIVITY DESIGNS
   Design 4-6 specific activities in full detail:

   For each activity, provide:
   - Activity name and type (individual, pair, small group, whole group)
   - Purpose and learning objective addressed
   - Step-by-step facilitation instructions (exact words to say)
   - Materials needed (with quantities and preparation notes)
   - Time allocation (including setup and debrief)
   - Grouping strategy and logistics
   - Debrief questions (minimum 3, increasing in depth)
   - Variations for different group sizes or settings
   - Common problems and solutions

6. FACILITATION TOOLKIT
   Provide ready-to-use facilitation tools:

   a. QUESTION BANK
      * Opening questions (to launch engagement)
      * Deepening questions (to push thinking)
      * Redirecting questions (to manage dominant voices)
      * Inclusive questions (to draw out quiet participants)
      * Closing questions (to synthesize learning)

   b. TRANSITION SCRIPTS
      * Between activities (bridges that maintain momentum)
      * After breaks (re-engagement techniques)
      * When discussions go off-track (gentle redirect)
      * When energy drops (engagement boosters)
      * When time runs short (graceful compression)

   c. DIFFICULT SITUATIONS
      * Handling a dominant participant
      * Managing conflict between participants
      * Dealing with technical failures
      * Addressing off-topic questions
      * Responding to "this is a waste of time"
      * Managing mixed-level groups

7. MATERIALS AND LOGISTICS
   - Complete materials list with preparation timeline
   - Room setup diagram (for in-person) or platform configuration (for virtual)
   - Technology requirements and backup plans
   - Participant handouts (ready to print or share)
   - Facilitator cheat sheet (single-page reference)
   - Pre-workshop communication template for participants

8. OUTCOME MEASUREMENT
   - Pre-workshop assessment (baseline measurement)
   - Real-time check-ins (pulse checks during the workshop)
   - Post-workshop assessment (immediate learning measurement)
   - Follow-up plan (30-day behavioral change measurement)
   - ROI indicators (what organizational outcomes this workshop drives)
   - Feedback form design (what to ask and how to analyze responses)

9. ADAPTATION GUIDE
   - How to scale this workshop up (larger group) or down (shorter time)
   - How to convert to virtual delivery
   - How to adapt for different cultural contexts
   - How to customize for different organizational levels (individual contributors vs. managers)
   - How to handle different room configurations

FORMAT as a complete facilitation package that a facilitator could pick up and deliver with minimal preparation. Include all scripts verbatim, all activities in full detail, and all materials ready to use. Write in an action-oriented, energetic tone that matches the workshop's intended energy.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
Design a 2-hour workshop on teamwork for corporate employees.
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
You are an expert workshop facilitator and learning experience designer with expertise in experiential learning, participatory facilitation, group dynamics, adult learning theory, and design thinking. Transform the following workshop concept into a complete, facilitated learning experience.

WORKSHOP: Teamwork and collaboration for corporate employees. 2-hour session. 20 participants (mixed departments, varying experience levels). Conference room with round tables (groups of 4-5). This is a quarterly professional development session. Management wants to improve cross-departmental collaboration.

ANALYZE THE INPUT:
- Topic: Teamwork and collaboration
- Audience: Corporate employees, mixed departments, 20 people
- Duration: 2 hours
- Setting: Conference room, round tables
- Context: Quarterly professional development, management-driven
- Goal: Improve cross-departmental collaboration

PRODUCE THE FOLLOWING WORKSHOP DESIGN:

1. WORKSHOP IDENTITY
2. LEARNING OUTCOMES
3. ENERGY AND ENGAGEMENT ARCHITECTURE
4. SESSION SEQUENCE (Minute-by-Minute)
5. ACTIVITY DESIGNS (4-6 specific activities)
6. FACILITATION TOOLKIT
7. MATERIALS AND LOGISTICS
8. OUTCOME MEASUREMENT
9. ADAPTATION GUIDE

FORMAT as a complete facilitation package ready to deliver. Include all scripts verbatim and all materials ready to use.
```

## Notes
- The energy architecture prevents the common mistake of scheduling passive content delivery throughout — energy must be managed deliberately.
- The engagement density rule (every 8-12 minutes) is based on adult attention research.
- Psychological safety is not a nice-to-have — it's the foundation of effective group learning.
- The facilitation toolkit is essential — even experienced facilitators need ready-to-use transitions and scripts.
- The outcome measurement section ensures the workshop produces results, not just satisfaction scores.
