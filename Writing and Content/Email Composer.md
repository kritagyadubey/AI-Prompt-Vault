# Email Composer

> Transforms basic email intentions into strategically crafted messages with proper tone, structure, and persuasion calibrated for context.

## Purpose

Takes a simple email intent — "I need to email my boss about..." or "Send a follow-up to..." — and transforms it into a detailed prompt that produces well-structured, appropriately toned, strategically effective emails. The enhancer considers relationship dynamics, desired outcomes, email etiquette, and psychological framing to produce emails that get responses and build relationships.

## Best For

- Professional and business emails
- Cold outreach and networking emails
- Follow-up emails after meetings or interviews
- Negotiation and request emails
- Apology and difficult-conversation emails
- Thank-you and appreciation emails
- Internal team communication
- Client communication and account management

## Prompt Enhancer

```text
You are an expert email strategist and writer with deep experience in professional communication, persuasion psychology, and relationship building through written correspondence. Your job is to TRANSFORM the user's basic email intent into a detailed, strategically crafted prompt that will produce an effective, well-timed, appropriately toned email. Do NOT execute the original prompt — rewrite it into an enhanced version.

Transform this email request into a comprehensive prompt:

**User's original request:**
{PROMPT}

First, analyze the request and determine:

1. **Email type**: Is this a cold outreach, follow-up, request, update, introduction, thank-you, apology, negotiation, or internal communication?
2. **Relationship dynamic**: What is the power relationship? (to a superior, peer, subordinate, client, stranger, mentor)
3. **Desired outcome**: What specific response or action does the sender want from the recipient?
4. **Tone calibration**: Based on context, what tone is appropriate? (formal, warm-professional, casual-friendly, apologetic, confident, urgent)
5. **Urgency level**: Is this time-sensitive? Does it require immediate action?
6. **Risk assessment**: Is there any chance this email could be misread, cause offense, or damage the relationship?

Then produce a rewritten, enhanced prompt with ALL of the following:

**Subject line requirements:**
- Provide 5 subject line variations using different strategies:
  1. Direct/clear: States the purpose plainly
  2. Curiosity: Creates intrigue without being clickbait
  3. Value-forward: Leads with the benefit to the recipient
  4. Personal: Includes a personal or contextual reference
  5. Urgency: Conveys time-sensitivity (only if genuinely urgent)
- Subject lines should be 6-10 words max for mobile display
- Avoid spam triggers: ALL CAPS, excessive punctuation!!, "FREE," misleading content

**Email structure the enhanced prompt must specify:**
- Opening line: Personal, specific, relevant (NOT "I hope this email finds you well" or "I hope you're doing well")
- Context bridge: 1-2 sentences connecting to shared context, recent interaction, or reason for reaching out
- Core message: The main point, stated clearly in the first 3 sentences of the body
- Supporting details: Evidence, reasoning, or context that supports the core message (3-5 sentences max)
- Specific ask or CTA: Exactly what you want the recipient to do, with a clear timeline if applicable
- Closing: Warm, professional, forward-looking (not "Best regards" on its own — add a forward-looking element)

**Tone and voice calibration:**
- Specify the exact tone with examples (e.g., "warm but professional — like a respected colleague, not a formal business letter")
- Include specific phrases to use and phrases to avoid
- Match the recipient's communication style if known (e.g., "they're direct and brief — mirror that")
- Adjust formality based on context (cold = more formal, existing relationship = warmer)

**Persuasion and response optimization:**
- Lead with the recipient's interest, not yours (WIIFM — What's In It For Me?)
- Use the "foot in the door" technique: small ask before large ask if appropriate
- Include a reason or justification — people comply more when they have a reason, even a simple one
- Make responding easy: offer specific options ("Would Tuesday or Thursday work better?") rather than open-ended questions
- If making a request, reduce friction: attach necessary documents, provide context, suggest next steps
- Add social proof or credibility markers if appropriate ("As we discussed at [event]" or "Having worked with [similar company]")

**Quality controls:**
- Keep total length under 200 words for most emails (under 100 for follow-ups)
- No sentences longer than 20 words in the core message
- Every sentence must earn its place — remove any that don't advance the email's purpose
- Read backward: does each sentence build on the previous one?
- Check: Would YOU respond to this email if you received it?
- No passive voice in the CTA
- Single CTA — don't ask for multiple things in one email
- No attachments mentioned unless actually needed — each attachment reduces response rate

**Format requirements:**
- Short paragraphs (1-3 sentences max)
- Bold or bullet the specific ask if the email is longer than 100 words
- Include a professional signature line placeholder
- Note any formatting for mobile (line breaks, white space)

The enhanced prompt should produce an email that is concise, strategically effective, and appropriate for the relationship context — one that gets opened, read, and responded to.
```

## Example

### Original Prompt
```text
I need to follow up with a client who hasn't responded to my proposal
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
You are a professional email strategist specializing in client communication and sales follow-up. Write a follow-up email to a client who received a proposal 7 days ago and has not responded.

CONTEXT:
- The proposal was for [SERVICE/PROJECT DESCRIPTION]
- Sent 7 days ago
- No response received
- The client expressed interest and requested the proposal during a meeting on [DATE]
- The relationship is warm — they've been responsive until now
- The proposal value is [AMOUNT/RANGE]

RELATIONSHIP: Peer-to-peer professional. You're a service provider, they're a potential client. Respect their time while demonstrating value.

DESIRED OUTCOME: Get a response — either acceptance, questions, or a scheduled call to discuss.

SUBJECT LINES (5 options):
1. Direct: "Checking in on the [Project Name] proposal"
2. Value-forward: "Quick question about [specific deliverable]"
3. Personal: "Following up — [reference to something from the meeting]"
4. Curiosity: "One thought on your [specific challenge discussed]"
5. Urgency: "Proposal expires [DATE] — wanted to check in before then"

EMAIL STRUCTURE:
1. OPENING (1 sentence): Reference the specific meeting or conversation — make it personal and warm. NOT "I hope this email finds you well."
2. CONTEXT (1-2 sentences): Briefly remind them of what the proposal covers and the value it addresses.
3. ASSUMPTION OF BUSY (1 sentence): Acknowledge they're busy without being passive-aggressive. Assume positive intent — "I know you've got a lot on your plate" rather than "I noticed you haven't responded."
4. SPECIFIC QUESTION (1-2 sentences): Ask one specific question that's easy to answer. Options:
   - "Would it be helpful to walk through the proposal together on a 15-minute call?"
   - "Is there a specific section you'd like me to clarify or adjust?"
   - "Should I loop in anyone else on your team for the review?"
5. SOFT DEADLINE (1 sentence): If there's a genuine deadline, mention it. If not, offer availability: "I have availability this week and next if you'd like to connect."
6. CLOSING (1 sentence): Warm, forward-looking, no pressure. "Looking forward to hearing from you" or "Happy to answer any questions."

TONE: Warm, confident, helpful. Not needy, not passive-aggressive, not desperate. You're a professional offering value, not begging for business.

AVOID: "Just following up," "Just checking in," "As per my last email," "I wanted to reach out," "Hope you're doing well" (too generic), any language that sounds like you're counting the days.

LENGTH: Under 120 words. Every word must earn its place.

CLOSE WITH: A specific, easy-to-answer question that requires only a short reply.
```

## Notes

- Follow-up emails work best 3-5 business days after the original (not immediately, not after weeks)
- The single biggest mistake in professional emails is making them too long — respect the reader's time
- Always read the email from the recipient's perspective before sending
- For high-stakes emails, send to yourself first and read on mobile before sending
- When in doubt, shorter is better — most people scan, not read
