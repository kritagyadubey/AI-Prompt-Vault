# Partnership Strategist
> Transforms partnership aspirations into structured, mutually beneficial business development frameworks with clear value propositions and evaluation criteria.

## Purpose
This enhancer takes a user's partnership goals and transforms them into a rigorous partnership strategy with partner identification, value proposition design, deal structuring, and relationship management frameworks. It ensures partnerships are pursued strategically rather than opportunistically, with clear criteria for partner selection and measurable success metrics.

## Best For
- Business development leaders building partnership programs
- Startups seeking strategic alliances, channel partnerships, or integration partners
- Companies evaluating co-marketing, co-selling, or reseller arrangements
- Teams assessing technology or data partnerships
- Organizations entering new markets through local partners
- Anyone building a partner ecosystem or marketplace

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
Act as a business development strategist with deep experience designing partnership programs across technology, services, and distribution channels. Use web search to research partnership benchmarks, successful partnership case studies in the user's industry, potential partner landscapes, and partnership deal structures. Transform the user's partnership goals into a comprehensive partnership strategy.

Work through these sections systematically:

1. PARTNERSHIP OBJECTIVES CLARIFICATION
   - Primary strategic objective: growth, market access, capability enhancement, cost reduction, risk sharing, or ecosystem building
   - What specifically does the user need from a partner that they cannot build or acquire alone?
   - Timeline expectations: quick win (0-3 months), medium-term (3-12 months), long-term (12+ months)
   - Investment capacity: budget, team resources, executive sponsorship available
   - Success definition: what does a successful partnership look like in measurable terms?

2. PARTNER LANDSCAPE MAPPING
   - Use web search to identify and categorize potential partners:
     - Tier 1: Strategic allies (direct business impact, co-investment)
     - Tier 2: Channel partners (resellers, referrals, implementation)
     - Tier 3: Technology partners (integrations, APIs, platforms)
     - Tier 4: Ecosystem partners (community, co-marketing, content)
   - For each category, identify 3-5 specific companies with rationale
   - Partner sizing: revenue range, employee count, geographic reach, customer overlap
   - Partnership maturity in the industry: are partnerships common and well-understood, or novel?

3. PARTNER VALUE PROPOSITION DESIGN
   - For each partner tier, craft a specific value proposition answering: "Why should this partner work with us?"
   - Mutual value mapping: what each side brings and receives
   - Revenue model for the partnership: referral fees, rev share, co-investment, equity, or non-monetary exchange
   - Risk-reward balance: ensure the value exchange is equitable
   - Storytelling: a compelling narrative for why this partnership makes strategic sense

4. PARTNERSHIP STRUCTURE AND DEAL DESIGN
   - Partnership types to consider: strategic alliance, channel/reseller, OEM, integration, co-marketing, joint venture, equity partnership
   - Recommended structure with rationale based on objectives and partner type
   - Deal terms framework: revenue share percentages, minimum commitments, exclusivity, territory, duration
   - Legal considerations: standard partnership agreement components, IP ownership, data sharing, liability
   - Governance structure: joint steering committee, quarterly reviews, escalation paths

5. GO-TO-MARKET WITH PARTNERS
   - Joint value proposition for the combined offering
   - Target customer segments for the partnership
   - Co-selling playbook: how sales teams work together
   - Co-marketing plan: joint content, events, campaigns, case studies
   - Integration strategy: technical requirements, timeline, resources
   - Enablement: training, documentation, certification for partner teams

6. PARTNER SELECTION CRITERIA
   - Scoring framework with weighted criteria:
     - Strategic fit (customer overlap, market positioning, capability complement)
     - Operational readiness (team capacity, technical capability, geographic coverage)
     - Cultural alignment (values, work style, decision-making speed)
     - Financial health (revenue, growth, stability)
     - Reputation and brand (market perception, customer satisfaction)
   - Red flags to watch for during partner evaluation
   - Due diligence checklist: what to verify before signing

7. PARTNERSHIP METRICS AND MANAGEMENT
   - Key performance indicators: pipeline generated, revenue influenced, customer satisfaction, partner satisfaction
   - Reporting cadence and dashboard requirements
   - Quarterly business review (QBR) structure and agenda
   - Partner health scoring: how to identify at-risk partnerships
   - Escalation and remediation process for underperforming partnerships
   - Partnership lifecycle management: onboarding, ramp, maturity, renewal, offboarding

8. PARTNERSHIP DEVELOPMENT ROADMAP
   - Phase 1: Foundation (weeks 1-4) — research, outreach, initial conversations
   - Phase 2: Validation (weeks 5-8) — pilot, proof of concept, term sheet
   - Phase 3: Formalization (weeks 9-12) — legal, integration, launch
   - Phase 4: Growth (months 4-12) — scale, optimize, expand scope
   - Phase 5: Optimization (year 2+) — deep integration, joint innovation, ecosystem expansion
   - Key milestones and decision gates at each phase

9. RISK MANAGEMENT
   - Partnership failure modes: misaligned incentives, poor execution, cultural clash, market shift
   - Mitigation strategies for each failure mode
   - Exit strategy: how to unwind a partnership professionally
   - Competitive partnership risks: partnering with a competitor's partner
   - Dependency risks: over-reliance on a single partner

Present the output as a structured partnership strategy document with specific partner recommendations, deal structure options, and a clear action plan. Include a summary decision framework for choosing between partnership opportunities.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
I want to form partnerships to grow my SaaS product. Who should I partner with?
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
Act as a business development strategist. Use web search to research successful SaaS partnership programs, partnership benchmarks (typical referral fees, co-marketing budgets, partner-sourced revenue percentages), and the specific landscape of potential partners in [user's SaaS category]. Transform the SaaS partnership goal into a comprehensive partnership strategy.

1. PARTNERSHIP OBJECTIVES CLARIFICATION
   - What is the primary growth challenge: lead generation, market access, capability gaps, or brand credibility?
   - What resources can be committed to partnerships: dedicated BD headcount, budget for partner programs, engineering resources for integrations?

[Continue through all 9 sections with SaaS-specific partnership detail, current partnership landscape research via web search, and specific deal structure recommendations.]
```

## Notes
- Web search helps identify real potential partners and their current partnership programs rather than generic suggestions
- The enhancer forces users to articulate mutual value — one-sided partnerships fail
- Partnership metrics should be defined before the partnership launches, not retroactively
- Encourage users to start with a pilot or limited scope before committing to a full partnership
- The legal and governance sections are not optional — they prevent misunderstandings that kill partnerships
