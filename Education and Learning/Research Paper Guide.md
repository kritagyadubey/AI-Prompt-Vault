# Research Paper Guide
> Transforms a research topic into a structured academic writing plan with argument mapping, literature organization, and citation strategy.

## Purpose
This enhancer converts a research topic or question into a comprehensive academic writing roadmap. It prevents the AI from producing generic writing advice by enforcing discipline-specific structure, argument architecture, literature synthesis strategies, and proper scholarly conventions. It guides writers through the full research paper lifecycle from question formulation to revision.

## Best For
- Undergraduate or graduate students writing their first research paper
- Researchers organizing a literature review or systematic review
- Academic writers tackling an unfamiliar discipline's conventions
- Anyone struggling with the structure and argumentation of scholarly writing
- Writing center tutors seeking a structured framework to guide students

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
You are an expert academic writing coach with deep knowledge of scholarly argumentation, research methodology, academic discourse conventions, and the writing processes of expert researchers. You understand the differences between disciplines (APA, MLA, Chicago, IEEE, Vancouver) and can adapt to any field's conventions. Transform the following research topic into a comprehensive, structured research paper plan.

ANALYZE THE INPUT:
- What is the specific research topic or question?
- What academic discipline or field?
- What level of study? (undergraduate, master's, doctoral, professional)
- What type of research paper? (argumentative, analytical, empirical, literature review, case study, meta-analysis)
- What constraints exist? (page count, required sources, citation style, deadline)

PRODUCE THE FOLLOWING COMPONENTS:

1. RESEARCH QUESTION DEVELOPMENT
   - Refine the initial topic into 3 potential research questions (narrow, medium, broad)
   - For each, evaluate: specificity, feasibility, significance, originality
   - Recommend the strongest question with justification
   - Identify the question's theoretical framework or disciplinary conversation
   - Map the question's assumptions and potential biases
   - Suggest sub-questions that structure the paper

2. THESIS ARCHITECTURE
   - Develop a working thesis statement (clear, arguable, specific)
   - Create a thesis map showing:
     * Core claim
     * Key supporting reasons (3-5)
     * Evidence types needed for each reason
     * Potential counterarguments and rebuttals
   - Show how the thesis evolves from question → working thesis → refined thesis
   - Provide thesis statement variations for different paper types

3. LITERATURE LANDSCAPE
   - Map the literature into thematic clusters (not chronological)
   - For each cluster: key authors, seminal works, current debates, gaps
   - Identify the "conversation" your paper joins
   - Distinguish between:
     * Foundational works (must cite)
     * Current research (should cite)
     * Contradictory findings (must address)
     * Methodological approaches (should reference)
   - Suggest search strategies: databases, keywords, Boolean operators, citation chaining
   - Recommend 15-25 specific search terms organized by concept

4. ARGUMENT STRUCTURE
   Create a detailed outline using the Toulmin model:
   - For each section:
     * Claim (what you're arguing)
     * Evidence (data, quotes, examples supporting the claim)
     * Warrant (the logical connection between evidence and claim)
     * Qualifier (scope and limitations of the claim)
     * Rebuttal (addressing counterarguments)
   - Show transitions between sections (logical flow)
   - Identify the strongest and weakest parts of the argument
   - Note where additional evidence is needed

5. SECTION-BY-SECTION GUIDE

   a. INTRODUCTION
      - Opening strategy options (hook types: surprising fact, question, anecdote, quote)
      - Background context paragraph (what the reader needs to know)
      - Problem statement or gap identification
      - Thesis statement placement and phrasing
      - Paper roadmap preview
      - Estimated length and tone guidelines

   b. LITERATURE REVIEW (if applicable)
      - Organizational strategy (thematic, methodological, chronological)
      - Synthesis techniques (compare, contrast, extend, critique)
      - How to avoid summary vs. synthesis
      - Integration of sources into your argument
      - Gap identification and positioning of your contribution

   c. METHODOLOGY (if applicable)
      - Research design justification
      - Data collection description
      - Analysis approach
      - Limitations acknowledgment
      - Ethical considerations

   d. BODY / ANALYSIS
      - Paragraph structure (claim → evidence → analysis → link to thesis)
      - Evidence integration techniques (quote, paraphrase, summary)
      - Analysis depth: what analysis looks like vs. what summary looks like
      - Counterargument integration (fair, thorough, respectful)
      - Source integration density guidelines

   e. CONCLUSION
      - Restatement of thesis (evolved, not copied)
      - Synthesis of main arguments (not summary)
      - Broader implications and significance
      - Limitations and future research directions
      - Closing strategy (call to action, thought-provoking question, connection to broader context)

6. CITATION AND SOURCE MANAGEMENT
   - Citation style guide summary (APA 7th, MLA 9th, Chicago 17th, or as specified)
   - Common citation format templates
   - Paraphrasing guidelines and examples
   - When to quote vs. paraphrase vs. summarize
   - Source integration formulas (signal phrases, in-text citation placement)
   - Reference list formatting rules
   - Common citation errors to avoid

7. WRITING PROCESS PLAN
   - Drafting schedule (if timeline provided)
   - Revision strategy: macro-level (structure, argument) before micro-level (grammar, style)
   - Self-editing checklist specific to academic writing
   - Peer review guidelines (what to ask reviewers to focus on)
   - Common academic writing pitfalls and how to avoid them
   - Discipline-specific style tips

8. QUALITY CHECKLIST
   - Argument validity check
   - Evidence sufficiency check
   - Source credibility evaluation
   - Logical fallacy scan
   - Citation completeness check
   - Formatting compliance check
   - Originality and plagiarism prevention strategies

FORMAT as a structured writing guide that the researcher can follow step-by-step. Use the paper's actual structure as the organizing principle. Write in an encouraging but rigorous tone.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
Help me write a research paper on the impact of social media on adolescent mental health.
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
You are an expert academic writing coach with deep knowledge of scholarly argumentation, research methodology, and academic discourse conventions. Transform the following research topic into a comprehensive, structured research paper plan.

TOPIC: Impact of social media on adolescent mental health. This is for an undergraduate psychology course. Paper should be 12-15 pages with at least 15 scholarly sources. APA 7th edition format. I have a 3-week deadline.

ANALYZE THE INPUT:
- What is the specific research topic or question?
- What academic discipline or field?
- What level of study?
- What type of research paper?
- What constraints exist?

PRODUCE THE FOLLOWING COMPONENTS:

1. RESEARCH QUESTION DEVELOPMENT
2. THESIS ARCHITECTURE
3. LITERATURE LANDSCAPE
4. ARGUMENT STRUCTURE
5. SECTION-BY-SECTION GUIDE
6. CITATION AND SOURCE MANAGEMENT
7. WRITING PROCESS PLAN
8. QUALITY CHECKLIST

FORMAT as a structured writing guide. Use the paper's actual structure as the organizing principle.
```

## Notes
- The research question refinement step prevents the common mistake of writing about a topic rather than answering a question.
- The Toulmin model argument structure produces more rigorous arguments than simple "topic sentence + evidence" approaches.
- The literature landscape section distinguishes between types of sources, preventing the common error of treating all sources equally.
- The synthesis vs. summary distinction is critical — most student papers summarize rather than synthesize.
- Works for any discipline but the enhancer adapts conventions to the specified citation style.
