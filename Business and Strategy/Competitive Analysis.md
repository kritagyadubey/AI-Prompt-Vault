# Competitive Analysis
> Transforms basic competitor awareness into a structured, data-driven competitive intelligence report with actionable strategic implications.

## Purpose
This enhancer takes a user's knowledge of their competitive landscape and transforms it into a rigorous, multi-dimensional competitive analysis. It goes beyond surface-level feature comparisons to examine competitor strategy, positioning, financial health, customer sentiment, hiring patterns, and market movements. It produces a structured report with clear implications for the user's strategy.

## Best For
- Product managers defining competitive positioning
- Marketing teams developing go-to-market differentiation
- Sales teams building battlecards and objection handling
- Founders evaluating market dynamics before entering a space
- Strategic planning teams conducting quarterly competitive reviews
- Business development teams assessing partnership opportunities

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
Act as a competitive intelligence analyst with deep experience in strategic analysis. Use web search extensively to gather current, accurate data about competitors — their products, pricing, funding, hiring, customer reviews, press coverage, technology stack, and market positioning. Do not rely on training data alone; verify claims with current sources.

Produce a comprehensive competitive analysis report with the following sections:

1. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE OVERVIEW
   - Total number of direct, indirect, and substitute competitors identified
   - Market concentration: fragmented, oligopoly, or dominated by a few players?
   - Competitive intensity assessment (1-10 scale with justification)
   - Key industry dynamics affecting competition (regulatory changes, technology shifts, consolidation trends)

2. COMPETITOR PROFILES (for each of the top 5-8 competitors)
   For each competitor, provide:
   - Company overview: founding date, headquarters, employee count, funding stage, last valuation
   - Product offering: core features, unique capabilities, limitations
   - Pricing model: structure, price points, free tier if any, enterprise pricing
   - Target market: who they serve (segment, size, geography)
   - Go-to-market approach: inbound vs outbound, channels used, partnerships
   - Strengths: top 3-5 specific strengths backed by evidence
   - Weaknesses: top 3-5 specific weaknesses drawn from customer reviews, analyst reports, or observable gaps
   - Recent developments: funding, product launches, leadership changes, strategic moves (last 12 months)
   - Customer sentiment: NPS ranges, review site ratings, common praise and complaints
   - Technology indicators: tech stack if observable, patent filings, open-source contributions

3. FEATURE COMPARISON MATRIX
   - Create a detailed table comparing your product vs. top 5-6 competitors
   - Include 15-25 feature dimensions across categories: core functionality, integrations, UX, pricing, support, security, scalability
   - Use clear indicators: full support, partial, not available, planned
   - Highlight your unique differentiators and areas where competitors outperform

4. PRICING ANALYSIS
   - Detailed pricing comparison across all competitors
   - Price-to-value assessment: who offers the best value at each tier?
   - Pricing trends: are competitors moving toward usage-based, seat-based, or hybrid models?
   - Opportunities for pricing differentiation

5. POSITIONING MAP
   - Define 2-3 positioning axes relevant to this market (e.g., ease-of-use vs. power, price vs. features, SMB vs. enterprise)
   - Map each competitor on these axes with justification
   - Identify positioning gaps or white spaces
   - Recommend optimal positioning for the user given their capabilities

6. CUSTOMER SENTIMENT ANALYSIS
   - Aggregate review data from G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, App Store, or relevant platforms
   - Common praise themes across competitors (what customers love)
   - Common complaint themes (what customers hate)
   - Unmet needs that represent opportunities
   - Switching patterns: why do customers leave competitors?

7. STRATEGIC IMPLICATIONS
   - Top 5 threats from the competitive landscape
   - Top 5 opportunities based on competitive gaps
   - Recommended competitive response strategies (short-term and long-term)
   - Battlecard summary: for each major competitor, one key advantage to highlight, one vulnerability to exploit, and one objection to preempt

8. MONITORING FRAMEWORK
   - Key signals to track for each competitor (product updates, hiring, funding, partnerships)
   - Recommended monitoring cadence (weekly, monthly, quarterly)
   - Tools and sources for ongoing competitive intelligence
   - Early warning indicators of competitive threat escalation

Present findings as a professional intelligence report with evidence citations. Distinguish between verified facts, strong inferences, and speculation. Flag areas where additional research is needed.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
Analyze my competitors in the project management software space.
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
Act as a competitive intelligence analyst. Use web search to gather current data on project management software competitors. Research Asana, Monday.com, Linear, Notion, ClickUp, Wrike, Smartsheet, and Basecamp — their current pricing (web search their pricing pages), recent product launches, funding and valuation, customer reviews on G2 and Capterra (latest 6 months), hiring patterns on LinkedIn, and press coverage.

Produce a comprehensive competitive analysis report:

1. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE OVERVIEW
   - How many project management tools exist? Is the market saturated or growing?
   - What is the competitive intensity? Are customers switching between tools frequently?
   - Key trends: AI integration, real-time collaboration, mobile-first design, integration ecosystems

[Continue through all 8 sections with specific, current data from web search, feature comparison matrix with 20+ dimensions, and actionable strategic implications.]
```

## Notes
- Web search is critical for this enhancer — competitive data changes rapidly and training data becomes outdated quickly
- The enhancer forces specificity: "they have good UX" becomes "their G2 ease-of-use score is 9.1 vs. industry average of 8.3"
- Encourage users to supplement AI analysis with direct customer interviews and win/loss data
- Distinguish between stated positioning (what competitors say) and revealed positioning (what their actual product, pricing, and reviews show)
- The monitoring framework section turns a one-time analysis into an ongoing capability
