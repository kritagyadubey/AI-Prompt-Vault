# Intellectual Property Advisor
> Transforms IP concerns into strategic frameworks covering patents, trademarks, copyrights, and trade secrets with filing strategies and enforcement plans.

## Purpose
This enhancer converts vague IP questions into comprehensive intellectual property strategy instructions that identify protectable assets, optimal protection mechanisms, filing strategies, and enforcement plans. It forces identification of IP types, ownership structures, filing timelines, and cost considerations that create actionable IP roadmaps rather than abstract protection concepts.

## Best For
- Startups identifying and protecting intellectual property assets
- Companies conducting IP audits and portfolio assessments
- Entrepreneurs deciding between patent, trademark, copyright, and trade secret protection
- Organizations navigating IP ownership disputes
- Businesses preparing for IP due diligence (M&A, fundraising)

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
I need you to act as an intellectual property strategist. Transform the following IP concern into a detailed, structured strategy instruction. DO NOT execute the strategy yourself — instead, produce a comprehensive prompt that instructs an AI to design an IP protection and enforcement strategy.

For the given IP situation, your enhanced prompt MUST include ALL of the following elements:

1. IP ASSET IDENTIFICATION AND CLASSIFICATION:
   - Inventory of all potentially protectable assets:
     * Inventions and technical innovations (patentable subject matter)
     * Brand names, logos, slogans, trade dress (trademarkable)
     * Original works of authorship (copyrightable)
     * Proprietary information, algorithms, processes (trade secret eligible)
   - Classification by IP type with protection mechanism mapping
   - Ownership analysis (inventorship, work-for-hire, joint development)
   - Prior art and existing rights landscape assessment
   - Freedom-to-operate considerations

2. PATENT STRATEGY (if applicable):
   - Patentability analysis framework:
     * Novelty assessment (35 U.S.C. § 102 / Art. 54 EPC)
     * Non-obviousness analysis (35 U.S.C. § 103 / Art. 56 EPC)
     * Utility/industrial applicability requirements
     * Subject matter eligibility (35 U.S.C. § 101 / Art. 52 EPC)
   - Filing strategy selection:
     * Provisional vs. non-provisional applications
     * PCT international filing timeline and national phase entry
     * Continuation, divisional, and CIP strategies
     * Design patent protection for ornamental features
   - Claim drafting strategy:
     * Broad independent claims with narrow dependent claims
     * Method vs. apparatus vs. system claims
     * Computer-implemented invention claiming strategies
   - Prosecution strategy and office action response planning
   - Maintenance and annuity payment schedules

3. TRADEMARK STRATEGY (if applicable):
   - Trademark selection criteria:
     * Distinctiveness spectrum (generic, descriptive, suggestive, arbitrary, fanciful)
     * Clearance search methodology (USPTO, state registries, common law)
     * International classification (Nice Agreement) selection
     * Domain name and social media handle availability
   - Filing strategy:
     * Use-based vs. intent-to-use applications
     * Federal (USPTO) vs. state registration
     * Madrid Protocol international filings
     * Section 44(d) and 44(e) priority claims
   - Brand protection program:
     * Monitoring and watch services
     * Opposition and cancellation proceedings
     * Customs recordation (import protection)
     * Online marketplace enforcement (Amazon Brand Registry, etc.)
   - Usage guidelines and proper trademark use requirements

4. COPYRIGHT STRATEGY (if applicable):
   - Protectability assessment:
     * Originality requirement (Feist standard)
     * Idea-expression dichotomy (17 U.S.C. § 102(b))
     * Fixed medium requirement
     * Work-for-hire doctrine application
   - Registration strategy:
     * Prompt registration benefits (statutory damages, attorney fees)
     * Group registration options
     * Deposit requirement compliance
     * Library of Congress procedures
   - Licensing framework:
     * Exclusive vs. non-exclusive license structures
     * Scope limitations (territory, duration, media, field of use)
     * Sublicensing rights
     * Derivative works authorization
   - Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) considerations
   - Fair use defense framework (purpose, nature, amount, market effect)

5. TRADE SECRET STRATEGY (if applicable):
   - Eligibility assessment:
     * Information type and economic value from secrecy
     * Reasonable measures to maintain secrecy
     * Derivation vs. independent development analysis
   - Protection program:
     * Employee/contractor confidentiality agreements
     * Non-disclosure agreement (NDA) templates and usage
     * Physical security measures (access controls, clean desk)
     * Digital security measures (encryption, access logging, DLP)
     * Information classification and marking system
   - Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA) considerations
   - Economic Espionage Act (EEA) protections
   - State Uniform Trade Secrets Act (UTSA) compliance
   - Exit interview and device return procedures

6. IP OWNERSHIP AND LICENSING:
   - Ownership determination:
     * Inventorship vs. ownership distinction
     * Assignment documentation requirements
     * Joint inventorship complications
     * University/institutional IP policies
     * Open source license implications
   - Licensing strategy:
     * Inbound vs. outbound licensing considerations
     * Cross-licensing arrangements
     * FRAND/RAND commitments (standards-essential patents)
     * Open source license compliance (GPL, MIT, Apache, etc.)
   - IP due diligence preparation:
     * Chain of title documentation
     * Assignment records and recorded interests
     * Pending litigation and encumbrances
     * IP valuation methodologies

7. ENFORCEMENT AND DEFENSE:
   - Infringement analysis framework:
     * Literal infringement vs. doctrine of equivalents (patents)
     * Likelihood of confusion analysis (trademarks)
     * Substantial similarity test (copyrights)
     * Misappropriation elements (trade secrets)
   - Enforcement strategy selection:
     * Cease and desist letter protocols
     * DMCA takedown notices
     * U.S. International Trade Commission (ITC) proceedings
     * Patent Trial and Appeal Board (PTAB) proceedings
     * Federal court litigation considerations
   - Defense strategies against IP claims:
     * Prior art invalidity contentions
     * Non-infringement arguments
     * Fair use and exhaustion doctrines
     * Laches and equitable defenses

8. BUDGET AND TIMELINE FRAMEWORK:
   - Cost estimation by IP type:
     * Patent: $8,000-$15,000+ per patent (prosecution through grant)
     * Trademark: $250-$400 per class (USPTO filing + attorney)
     * Copyright: $45-$65 per registration (online filing)
     * Trade secret: Program implementation costs (variable)
   - Timeline expectations:
     * Patent: 2-4 years to grant (utility), 12-18 months (design)
     * Trademark: 8-12 months to registration (if no opposition)
     * Copyright: 3-9 months for registration certificate
   - Maintenance and renewal schedules
   - Portfolio management budget allocation

9. INTERNATIONAL IP PROTECTION:
   - Priority filing strategies (Paris Convention 12-month/6-month)
   - PCT and Madrid Protocol considerations
   - Country-specific requirements and pitfalls
   - Enforcement mechanisms in target markets
   - IP customs enforcement and border measures

INSTRUCTIONS FOR THE AI:
- Map each IP asset to optimal protection mechanisms
- Include specific statutory and regulatory references
- Provide filing timeline visualizations where helpful
- Flag common pitfalls and mistakes to avoid
- Include cost-benefit analysis framework for protection decisions
- Add an "IP PORTFOLIO SUMMARY" template
- Note that IP strategy should be validated by a registered patent attorney/trademark attorney

DISCLAIMER: This is an IP strategy instruction, not legal advice. Intellectual property matters require consultation with qualified IP attorneys registered in the relevant jurisdiction. Patent matters require a registered patent attorney or agent before the USPTO.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
I have a new software invention and want to protect my intellectual property. What should I do?
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
I need you to act as an intellectual property strategist. Transform the following into a detailed IP strategy instruction for [INSERT COMPANY NAME], a [INSERT STAGE: pre-seed/seed/series A] startup in [INSERT INDUSTRY] with [INSERT NUMBER] founders.

Our software invention is: [INSERT DETAILED DESCRIPTION OF THE SOFTWARE INVENTION, INCLUDING KEY FEATURES, ALGORITHMS, AND TECHNICAL IMPROVEMENTS].

Design an IP protection strategy covering: (1) Patent eligibility analysis under 35 U.S.C. § 101 (Alice/Mayo framework for software patents); (2) Patentability search strategy with specific databases and search queries; (3) Provisional vs. non-provisional filing decision framework; (4) PCT international filing strategy targeting [INSERT TARGET MARKETS]; (5) Trade secret protection for algorithms not suitable for patenting; (6) Copyright registration for software code and documentation; (7) Trademark selection and clearance for [INSERT BRAND NAME]; (8) Open source license compliance for third-party dependencies; (9) IP ownership documentation (assignment agreements, joint development); (10) Budget framework with cost estimates; (11) Filing timeline with critical deadlines; (12) IP due diligence preparation for fundraising. Include specific statutory references, cost estimates, and timeline visualizations. DO NOT execute the strategy — produce the enhanced prompt above.
```

## Notes
- IP protection is time-sensitive — filing dates matter
- Provisional patents provide 12 months to file non-provisional applications
- Trade secrets require active protection measures — they don't protect themselves
- Copyright exists upon creation but registration is required for enforcement
- Trademark rights can be lost through genericide or abandonment
- Open source licenses have specific compliance obligations
- IP ownership should be documented before any public disclosure
- International IP protection requires separate filings in each jurisdiction
- Patent costs can be significant — budget accordingly
