# Change Management
> Transforms change initiatives into structured, people-centered implementation plans with communication strategies and resistance management.

## Purpose
This enhancer takes a change initiative and transforms it into a comprehensive change management plan that addresses the people side of change. It covers stakeholder analysis, communication planning, resistance management, training, and measurement. It ensures changes are adopted successfully, not just implemented.

## Best For
- Leaders planning organizational restructuring or strategy pivots
- IT teams implementing new systems or processes
- HR teams driving culture change or policy updates
- Project managers rolling out new workflows or tools
- Change management professionals building structured programs
- Any leader who needs people to adopt new behaviors or ways of working

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
Act as an organizational change management expert with deep expertise in Kotter's 8-Step Model, ADKAR framework, and Prosci methodology. Use web search to research change management best practices, industry benchmarks for change adoption rates, and case studies relevant to the user's type of change. Transform the change initiative into a comprehensive, people-centered change management plan.

Work through these sections systematically:

1. CHANGE CHARACTERIZATION
   - What exactly is changing? Describe the current state and desired future state in specific, observable terms
   - Type of change: process, technology, structure, strategy, culture, or a combination
   - Scope and scale: team, department, division, or enterprise-wide
   - Complexity assessment: simple, complicated, or complex (Cynefin framework)
   - Timeline: when does change need to be fully adopted?
   - Business case: why is this change necessary now? What is the cost of NOT changing?

2. STAKEHOLDER ANALYSIS
   - Identify all stakeholder groups affected by the change
   - For each group, assess: impact level (high/medium/low), influence level (high/medium/low), current attitude (supportive/neutral/resistant), and information needs
   - Stakeholder mapping matrix: who needs to be managed closely, kept satisfied, kept informed, or monitored
   - Executive sponsor identification and role definition
   - Change champion network: identify potential advocates in each affected group
   - Voice of the customer: how will you gather and incorporate employee feedback?

3. ADKAR ASSESSMENT AND STRATEGY
   For each ADKAR element, assess current state and design interventions:
   - AWARENESS: Do people understand why the change is happening? What communication is needed?
   - DESIRE: Do people want to participate? What motivates each stakeholder group?
   - KNOWLEDGE: Do people know how to change? What training is required?
   - ABILITY: Can people implement the change? What practice and support is needed?
   - REINFORCEMENT: How will the change be sustained? What prevents regression?

4. COMMUNICATION PLAN
   - Communication objectives for each phase (awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement)
   - Key messages tailored to each stakeholder group (what's in it for them)
   - Communication channels: town halls, team meetings, email, intranet, Slack/Teams, videos, FAQs
   - Communication cadence: weekly, bi-weekly, monthly touchpoints
   - Two-way communication: feedback mechanisms (surveys, focus groups, office hours, anonymous channels)
   - Communication timeline: what to communicate, when, through which channel, from whom
   - Transparency guidelines: what can be shared, what must be held confidential, and how to handle uncertainty

5. RESISTANCE MANAGEMENT
   - Anticipate resistance: what are the top 5 likely objections or concerns?
   - Root cause analysis: is resistance due to lack of information, past change fatigue, loss of control, fear of incompetence, or disagreement with the change itself?
   - Response strategies for each type of resistance:
     - Information resistance: provide data, context, rationale
     - Emotional resistance: listen, empathize, acknowledge loss
     - Political resistance: engage influencers, address power dynamics
     - Capability resistance: provide training, practice, support
   - Early warning indicators: how to detect resistance before it escalates
   - Intervention triggers: when to escalate from manager-led to change team-led responses
   - Adaptation plan: how to modify the change based on legitimate feedback

6. TRAINING AND ENABLEMENT
   - Skills gap analysis: what new skills or knowledge are required?
   - Training design: classroom, e-learning, on-the-job, coaching, blended
   - Training timeline: when does each group need to be trained relative to go-live?
   - Role-specific training paths: different groups need different training
   - Practice and rehearsal: opportunities to practice new behaviors before they're required
   - Support infrastructure: help desks, champions, documentation, job aids
   - Competency assessment: how to verify people are ready to operate in the new state

7. IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP
   - Phase 1: Preparing for change (weeks 1-4) — sponsorship alignment, stakeholder analysis, communication prep
   - Phase 2: Managing the change (weeks 5-12) — communication launch, training delivery, early adoption
   - Phase 3: Reinforcing the change (weeks 13-24) — measurement, course correction, celebration
   - Key milestones and decision gates
   - Resource requirements: people, budget, tools, time
   - Quick wins: what early successes can demonstrate the value of the change?
   - Risk register: top 5 risks with probability, impact, and mitigation

8. MEASUREMENT AND SUSTAINABILITY
   - Change adoption metrics: usage rates, compliance, behavioral indicators
   - Business impact metrics: how does the change affect KPIs?
   - People metrics: employee satisfaction, engagement, attrition during and after change
   - Measurement cadence: daily, weekly, monthly tracking
   - Sustainability mechanisms: how to prevent regression to old ways
   - Continuous improvement: how to evolve the change based on feedback and results
   - Success criteria: what does "done" look like and how will you know when you've arrived?

9. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
   - Executive sponsor: role, time commitment, specific actions
   - Change management team: composition, responsibilities, decision authority
   - Middle managers: their critical role in change adoption
   - Change champions: selection criteria, training, recognition
   - HR and L&D: support role in training and culture
   - Communications team: support role in messaging and channels

Present the output as a structured change management plan with clear phases, responsible parties, timelines, and success metrics. Include a change readiness assessment template that can be used before launch.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
We're switching from Slack to Microsoft Teams. Help me manage the transition.
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
Act as an organizational change management expert. Use web search to research change management best practices for technology migrations, Slack-to-Teams migration case studies, typical adoption challenges, and benchmarks for technology change adoption rates. Transform the Slack-to-Teams migration into a comprehensive change management plan.

1. CHANGE CHARACTERIZATION
   - Current state: team's current Slack usage patterns, integrations, workflows, channels, culture around Slack
   - Desired state: fully adopted Teams environment with equivalent or better functionality
   - What specifically is changing: tool, workflows, integrations, search history, bots, file sharing
   - What is NOT changing: team structures, processes that don't depend on the messaging tool

[Continue through all 9 sections with Slack-to-Teams-specific detail, including migration-specific resistance points, training needs, and timeline.]
```

## Notes
- Web search helps find relevant case studies and benchmarks for the specific type of change being managed
- The enhancer emphasizes that change management is about people, not just process or technology
- Resistance should be anticipated and planned for, not treated as an obstacle
- Quick wins are critical for building momentum and credibility
- The plan should be iterative — adjust based on feedback and measurement data
