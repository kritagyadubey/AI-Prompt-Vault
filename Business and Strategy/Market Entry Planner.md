# Market Entry Planner
> Transforms market expansion goals into structured entry strategies with localization, regulatory, and go-to-market roadmaps.

## Purpose
This enhancer takes a company's desire to enter a new market and transforms it into a comprehensive market entry strategy. It covers market selection rationale, entry mode analysis, regulatory assessment, localization requirements, competitive landscape in the new market, go-to-market sequencing, and financial projections for the expansion. It ensures market entry decisions are evidence-based, not aspirational.

## Best For
- Companies expanding into new geographic markets (domestic or international)
- Product teams launching into new customer segments or verticals
- Startups evaluating which market to enter first
- Business development leaders evaluating market entry through partnerships or acquisitions
- Any organization considering a significant market expansion
- Teams assessing regulatory feasibility before committing resources

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
Act as a market entry strategist with deep experience in international expansion, vertical market entry, and go-to-market planning. Use web search extensively to research the target market's size, growth, competitive landscape, regulatory environment, cultural considerations, and consumer behavior. Transform the market expansion goal into a comprehensive market entry strategy.

Work through these sections systematically:

1. MARKET OPPORTUNITY ASSESSMENT
   - Target market definition: geography, customer segment, or vertical
   - Market size estimation: TAM, SAM, SOM for the target market specifically (web search for market data)
   - Market growth rate and trajectory
   - Market maturity: emerging, growing, mature, or declining
   - Key market drivers: what is creating the opportunity now?
   - Market barriers: what makes this market difficult to enter?
   - Timing analysis: why now? What window of opportunity exists?

2. ENTRY MODE ANALYSIS
   Evaluate and recommend from these options:
   - DIRECT ENTRY: establishing local presence (own office, team, operations)
   - PARTNERSHIP: joint venture, strategic alliance, distribution agreement
   - ACQUISITION: buying an existing player in the market
   - LICENSING/FRANCHISING: allowing a local entity to use your brand and model
   - ONLINE/DIGITAL: serving the market remotely without physical presence
   - PHASED ENTRY: starting with a beachhead and expanding
   
   For each mode: cost estimate, time to revenue, risk level, control level, resource requirements, and recommendation rationale.

3. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE IN TARGET MARKET
   - Use web search to identify local and global competitors in the target market
   - Market share distribution: who dominates and why?
   - Competitor strengths and weaknesses specific to this market
   - Customer switching costs and behaviors
   - Pricing benchmarks in the market
   - Distribution channels and partnerships competitors use
   - Unmet needs or underserved segments

4. REGULATORY AND LEGAL ASSESSMENT
   - Business registration and licensing requirements
   - Industry-specific regulations and compliance requirements
   - Foreign ownership restrictions or requirements
   - Tax implications: corporate tax, VAT/GST, transfer pricing
   - Data privacy and protection regulations (GDPR, local equivalents)
   - Employment law considerations: hiring, termination, benefits
   - Intellectual property protection in the target market
   - Import/export regulations if applicable
   - Timeline and cost for regulatory compliance

5. LOCALIZATION STRATEGY
   - Language localization: translation, transcreation, content adaptation
   - Cultural adaptation: business practices, communication styles, holidays, values
   - Product adaptation: what needs to change for the local market?
   - Payment methods: local payment preferences and infrastructure
   - Customer support: local language, time zones, support expectations
   - Marketing and brand adaptation: messaging, channels, imagery
   - Legal document localization: contracts, terms of service, privacy policy

6. GO-TO-MARKET PLAN
   - Beachhead strategy: where to start within the target market
   - Customer acquisition channels ranked for this specific market
   - Sales model: direct, channel, or hybrid
   - Pricing for the local market: currency, price sensitivity, competitive positioning
   - Partnership and distribution strategy: local partners, resellers, distributors
   - Marketing plan: channels, messaging, budget allocation, cultural considerations
   - Launch timeline: pre-launch, launch, post-launch phases

7. OPERATIONAL REQUIREMENTS
   - Team: roles needed locally vs. remotely, hiring timeline, cost estimates
   - Infrastructure: office, technology, tools, systems needed
   - Supply chain: if physical product, local sourcing, logistics, fulfillment
   - Technology: local hosting, CDN, payment processing, compliance
   - Vendor selection: local service providers (legal, accounting, HR, marketing)
   - Quality assurance: how to maintain standards in a new market

8. FINANCIAL PROJECTIONS
   - Entry costs: one-time setup costs with specific estimates
   - Ongoing costs: monthly/annual operating costs in the new market
   - Revenue projections: conservative, base, optimistic scenarios
   - Break-even timeline: when does the market become profitable?
   - ROI analysis: return on the investment required to enter
   - Currency risk: exposure and hedging strategies
   - Funding requirements: how will the expansion be funded?

9. RISK ASSESSMENT AND MITIGATION
   - Market risks: demand uncertainty, competitive response, pricing pressure
   - Operational risks: execution complexity, talent availability, quality control
   - Financial risks: currency, cash flow, funding gaps
   - Regulatory risks: compliance failures, regulatory changes, legal disputes
   - Cultural risks: miscommunication, brand damage, relationship failures
   - Mitigation strategies for each high-priority risk
   - Kill criteria: what conditions would trigger withdrawal from the market?

10. IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP
    - Phase 1: Research and validation (weeks 1-6)
    - Phase 2: Setup and compliance (weeks 7-12)
    - Phase 3: Pre-launch (weeks 13-16)
    - Phase 4: Launch (weeks 17-20)
    - Phase 5: Post-launch optimization (weeks 21+)
    - Key milestones and decision gates at each phase
    - Success metrics: what does a successful market entry look like at 90 days, 6 months, 12 months?

Present the output as a comprehensive market entry strategy document with specific market data from web search, clear go/no-go criteria, and an actionable implementation plan. Include a market entry decision matrix comparing the top 2-3 market options.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
We want to expand our SaaS product into the European market. How should we approach it?
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
Act as a market entry strategist. Use web search to research the European SaaS market by country (UK, Germany, France, Netherlands, Nordics), GDPR requirements for SaaS companies, European payment preferences, data residency requirements, competitive landscape in each market, typical SaaS pricing in Europe, and successful US-to-Europe SaaS expansion case studies. Design a comprehensive European market entry strategy.

1. MARKET OPPORTUNITY ASSESSMENT
   - Which European country should be the beachhead? Compare UK, Germany, France, Nordics on: market size, English proficiency, SaaS adoption, ease of doing business, regulatory complexity
   - What is the total European SaaS TAM for this product category?
   - What are the key growth drivers in European SaaS?

[Continue through all 10 sections with Europe-specific detail including GDPR compliance, data residency, localization requirements, and country-specific go-to-market approaches.]
```

## Notes
- Web search is essential for current market data, regulatory requirements, and competitive landscape in the target market
- The enhancer forces rigorous market selection rather than impulse-driven expansion
- Regulatory assessment is not optional — non-compliance can be catastrophic
- Localization goes beyond translation — cultural adaptation is critical for market acceptance
- Financial projections should include all entry costs, not just ongoing operations
