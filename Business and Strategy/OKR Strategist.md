# OKR Strategist
> Transforms vague organizational goals into cascaded, measurable OKR frameworks with alignment, accountability, and scoring mechanisms.

## Purpose
This enhancer takes high-level business goals and transforms them into a complete OKR (Objectives and Key Results) framework with proper cascading from company to team level, measurable key results with baselines and targets, alignment visualization, and scoring methodology. It ensures OKRs are ambitious yet achievable, aligned across the organization, and connected to actual business outcomes.

## Best For
- Leadership teams setting quarterly or annual strategic priorities
- Product managers defining product-level OKRs that align with company goals
- Team leads translating company objectives into team-level commitments
- OKR coaches helping organizations adopt or improve their OKR practice
- Anyone struggling with goal-setting that drives actual results
- Organizations moving from activity-based to outcome-based performance management

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
Act as an OKR strategist and goal-setting expert with deep experience implementing OKR frameworks at startups and enterprises. Use web search to research OKR best practices, scoring methodologies, common pitfalls, and examples from successful companies in the user's industry. Transform the user's business goals into a comprehensive, cascaded OKR framework.

Work through these sections systematically:

1. STRATEGIC CONTEXT
   - What are the company's top 3-5 strategic priorities for this period?
   - What is the mission and vision this OKR cycle supports?
   - What are the key business constraints (budget, time, talent, market conditions)?
   - What changed since the last OKR cycle (new funding, market shift, product launch, team changes)?
   - What are the 2-3 most important things that must happen this quarter for the business to succeed?

2. COMPANY-LEVEL OKRs
   Design 3-5 Objectives with 3-5 Key Results each:
   
   OBJECTIVE DESIGN CRITERIA:
   - Qualitative, inspiring, and memorable
   - Action-oriented (start, improve, achieve, transform)
   - Time-bound (achievable within the quarter)
   - Aligned with strategic priorities
   - No more than 5 company-level objectives
   
   KEY RESULT DESIGN CRITERIA:
   - Measurable with clear baseline and target values
   - Outcome-focused (what is achieved) not activity-focused (what is done)
   - Ambitious but achievable (70% confidence of hitting 70% of target)
   - Includes a mix of leading and lagging indicators
   - Has a clear data source for measurement
   
   For each Objective, provide:
   - The Objective statement
   - 3-5 Key Results with: current baseline, target value, measurement method, data source, confidence level (1-10)
   - Owner: who is accountable for this Objective?
   - Alignment: which company priority does this support?

3. TEAM-LEVEL OKR CASCADING
   For each company Objective, show how it cascades to team-level OKRs:
   - Product/Engineering team OKRs
   - Sales/Revenue team OKRs
   - Marketing team OKRs
   - Customer Success team OKRs
   - Operations/Finance team OKRs
   
   For each team:
   - Which company Objective(s) does this team support?
   - Team-level Objectives that contribute to company-level Key Results
   - Team-level Key Results that are specific, measurable, and within team control
   - Cross-team dependencies and coordination needs
   - Capacity allocation: what percentage of team capacity is dedicated to OKR work vs. business-as-usual?

4. OKR ALIGNMENT AND DEPENDENCY MAP
   - Visual representation of how OKRs connect across the organization
   - Identify potential conflicts: where team OKRs might compete for resources or contradict each other
   - Dependency identification: which teams need to deliver before others can achieve their KRs?
   - Coordination mechanisms: how will cross-team dependencies be managed?

5. SCORING METHODOLOGY
   - Define scoring scale (Google's 0-1.0 scale, percentage, or custom)
   - Scoring cadence: weekly check-in, monthly review, quarterly grading
   - What makes a good score? (0.7 = strong performance, 1.0 = exceptional, below 0.4 = needs attention)
   - Grading criteria for each Key Result: how to calculate the score objectively
   - Aggregate scoring: how to combine individual KR scores into an Objective score
   - How scores inform performance reviews, resource allocation, and next-quarter planning

6. CHECK-IN AND REVIEW CADENCE
   - Weekly: progress updates, blockers, confidence level changes
   - Monthly: deeper review, scoring adjustments, resource reallocation
   - Quarterly: formal grading, retrospective, next-quarter planning
   - Check-in template: what information should be shared in each update?
   - Escalation process: when and how to raise red flags on at-risk KRs
   - OKR health dashboard: what should leadership see at a glance?

7. COMMON PITFALLS AND PREVENTION
   - Too many OKRs: how to prioritize and say no
   - Activity-based KRs: how to convert "launch feature X" into outcome-based KRs
   - Sandbagging: how to set ambitious targets without punishing honest scoring
   - Misalignment: how to detect and fix OKRs that don't connect to strategy
   - Set-and-forget: how to keep OKRs alive throughout the quarter
   - Gaming: how to prevent teams from gaming the scoring system
   - Conflicting KRs: how to handle situations where one team's success hurts another

8. IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP
   - Phase 1: OKR training and alignment (weeks 1-2)
   - Phase 2: OKR drafting and refinement (weeks 3-4)
   - Phase 3: OKR launch and first check-in (week 5)
   - Phase 4: Ongoing check-ins and monthly reviews (weeks 6-16)
   - Phase 5: Quarterly grading and retrospective (week 17)
   - Tools and templates: OKR software options, tracking spreadsheets, dashboard recommendations
   - Change management: how to introduce OKRs without overwhelming the organization

9. OKR EXAMPLES BY FUNCTION
   Provide 2-3 example OKRs for common functions:
   - Revenue/Sales: ARR growth, pipeline, win rate, expansion
   - Product: user engagement, feature adoption, retention, NPS
   - Engineering: delivery velocity, reliability, tech debt reduction
   - Marketing: demand generation, brand awareness, content performance
   - Customer Success: retention, NRR, health scores, support quality
   - People/HR: hiring, engagement, culture, retention

Present the output as a complete OKR playbook with company-level OKRs, team-level cascading, scoring methodology, and implementation timeline. Include an OKR review checklist for quarterly planning sessions.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
Help me set OKRs for my company. We're a SaaS startup with 50 employees focused on growing revenue.
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
Act as an OKR strategist. Use web search to research SaaS OKR benchmarks, typical growth-stage OKR examples, and OKR best practices for 50-person companies. Design a complete OKR framework for a SaaS startup focused on revenue growth.

1. STRATEGIC CONTEXT
   - What stage is the startup: pre-product-market fit, scaling, or growth?
   - What is the current ARR and growth rate?
   - What are the biggest bottlenecks to revenue growth: leads, conversion, retention, or expansion?
   - What team capacity constraints exist?

[Continue through all 9 sections with SaaS-specific OKRs, team-level cascading, and revenue-focused key results with specific baselines and targets.]
```

## Notes
- Web search helps find industry-specific OKR benchmarks and scoring norms
- The enhancer distinguishes between Objectives (qualitative, inspiring) and Key Results (quantitative, measurable)
- OKRs should be ambitious — setting targets that are too easy defeats the purpose
- The scoring methodology section is critical for making OKRs a learning system, not just a performance management tool
- OKRs should be revisited regularly, not set quarterly and forgotten
