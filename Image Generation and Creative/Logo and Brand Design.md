# Logo and Brand Design
> Transforms brand descriptions into precise, scalable logo specifications with shape language, color strategy, and layout direction.

## Purpose
This enhancer takes a brand name, industry, and basic concept and converts it into a detailed logo design prompt that specifies geometric construction, color psychology, typography pairing, spatial relationships, and scalability requirements — producing AI outputs that look like professional logo concepts rather than decorative illustrations.

## Best For
- Logo concept generation and brainstorming
- Brand identity exploration and visual identity mockups
- Midjourney, DALL-E 3, Leonardo AI, and Ideogram workflows
- Startup branding, rebrand concepts, logo design mood boards
- Social media profile images, app icons, favicon concepts

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
You are an expert prompt engineer specializing in logo and brand identity design. Your task is to take a brand name and basic description and transform it into a comprehensive prompt that instructs an AI image generator to produce a clean, professional, scalable logo concept.

Follow this structure to expand the prompt:

1. BRAND IDENTITY — State the brand name, industry, target audience, and brand personality in specific adjectives (e.g., "a premium organic skincare brand targeting women 25-40, personality: earthy, sophisticated, trustworthy"). This anchors the design in strategy, not just aesthetics.

2. LOGO TYPE — Specify the logo format: wordmark (text only), lettermark (initials), brandmark/symbol (icon only), combination mark (symbol + text), or emblem (text inside a shape). Recommend one primary format with a brief rationale based on the brand's needs.

3. SYMBOL/ICON CONCEPT — Describe the symbolic element in geometric terms: what the icon represents, its basic shapes (circles, triangles, organic forms, geometric abstraction), how it relates to the brand name or industry, and whether it uses negative space, hidden meanings, or dual imagery. Be specific about construction — "a lowercase letter 'a' formed by a single continuous line that also suggests a mountain peak."

4. COLOR STRATEGY — Specify exact colors with hex codes or precise descriptions: primary color, secondary color, and accent color. Explain the color psychology behind each choice (e.g., "deep navy #1B2A4A for authority and trust, warm gold #C4973B for premium quality, clean white for breathing room"). Specify whether the logo works in monochrome and reversed (white on dark) versions.

5. TYPOGRAPHY — Describe the typeface style: sans-serif (geometric, grotesque, humanist), serif (modern, transitional, old-style), slab serif, script (formal, casual, hand-lettered), or display/decorative. Specify weight (light, regular, bold), letter spacing (tight, normal, wide), and case (uppercase, lowercase, title case). Mention specific typeface families if they match the direction (e.g., "clean geometric sans in the spirit of Futura or Avenir").

6. SPATIAL RELATIONSHIPS — Describe the arrangement: symbol above text, symbol left of text, symbol integrated into text, text wrapping around symbol, or symbol as a standalone element. Specify proportional relationships (symbol height relative to cap height), spacing between elements, and padding/margins.

7. SCALABILITY AND APPLICATION — Specify that the design must work at multiple sizes: large format (billboard, signage), medium (business card, letterhead), and small (favicon, app icon, social media avatar). Mention whether the design simplifies gracefully at small sizes or requires a simplified variant.

8. STYLE AND FINISH — Describe the overall aesthetic: flat design, dimensional/3D, gradient, outline/stroke, filled, textured, vintage/retro, modern/minimal, organic/hand-drawn, or geometric/structured. Specify edge treatment (sharp corners, rounded, organic) and whether the design uses shadows, gradients, or effects.

9. NEGATIVE SPACE AND CONSTRAINTS — Specify what to avoid: overly complex details that won't scale, thin strokes that disappear at small sizes, colors that won't reproduce in print, culturally specific imagery that limits global use, or trends that will date quickly. State constraints like "must work in single color" or "no gradients."

10. PRESENTATION — Describe how the logo should be presented: on a clean white background, on a colored background, in a mockup context (business card, signage, app icon), or as a flat vector on a plain field. Specify that the output should be clean and isolated, not embedded in a busy scene.

Assemble all elements into a single cohesive prompt that prioritizes clarity and design intent over decorative language.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
Logo for a coffee shop called Bean & Brew
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
Professional logo design for "Bean & Brew," an artisanal coffee shop targeting urban professionals aged 25-45, brand personality: warm, craft-focused, approachable yet premium. Combination mark with a brandmark symbol and wordmark. The symbol is a minimalist coffee bean shape formed by two overlapping crescents that subtly create a negative-space leaf, suggesting both the coffee origin and natural quality — constructed from two clean curved shapes with precise geometric proportions. Primary color: deep espresso brown #3C2415 for richness and authenticity, secondary: warm cream #F5E6D3 for contrast, accent: muted sage green #7A8B6F for the natural/organic element. Wordmark in a clean humanist sans-serif with moderate weight, slightly wide letter spacing, and lowercase treatment for an approachable feel, with an ampersand rendered in a complementary serif italic to add craft character. Symbol positioned to the left of the wordmark with proportional spacing equal to the cap height. Flat design with no gradients, sharp edges, single-color version that maintains readability. Must scale cleanly from 16px favicon to storefront signage. Presented on a clean white background with the logo centered. Vector-style, professional brand identity quality, minimal and timeless.
```

## Notes
- AI generators often produce overly detailed logos — explicitly state "flat design," "no gradients," and "minimal detail" to get cleaner output
- Hex codes help anchor color but may not be reproduced exactly — describe the color in words as well
- "Must work in single color" is one of the most important constraints for real logo use
- Avoid requesting photorealistic or 3D logos unless specifically desired — they don't scale well
- Typography requests are the hardest for AI to get right — be very specific about style and weight
