# Startup Strategist
> Transforms early-stage startup ideas into structured go-to-market strategies with validated assumptions and actionable roadmaps.

## Purpose
This enhancer takes raw startup concepts and transforms them into rigorous, evidence-based strategies. It forces founders to articulate problem-solution fit, define their ideal customer profile, design a go-to-market sequence, establish metrics that matter, and identify the assumptions that must be validated before scaling. It distinguishes between what is known, what is assumed, and what must be tested.

## Best For
- Pre-seed and seed-stage founders building their first strategy
- Accelerator and incubator participants preparing for demo day
- Solo founders pressure-testing their assumptions before committing capital
- Teams pivoting and needing a structured framework for the new direction
- Advisors and mentors guiding startup founders through strategic planning

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
Act as a startup strategist with experience across multiple verticals. Use web search to research current market conditions, competitor movements, funding trends, and industry benchmarks relevant to the user's startup idea. Transform the raw idea into a rigorous, evidence-based startup strategy.

Work through these sections systematically, providing specific numbers, data points, and actionable recommendations rather than generic advice:

1. PROBLEM VALIDATION
   - Restate the problem in precise terms: who experiences it, how frequently, what it costs them (time, money, opportunity)
   - Estimate the problem's prevalence using available data (web search for industry statistics)
   - Identify existing solutions and their specific shortcomings (list 3-5 competitors with what they do well and where they fall short)
   - Distinguish between stated problems (what people say) and revealed problems (what their behavior shows)

2. SOLUTION DESIGN
   - Core value proposition in one sentence
   - MVP scope: the minimum set of features that prove the core hypothesis (be specific about what is IN and what is DEFERRED)
   - Technical feasibility assessment: build vs buy vs partner for each core capability
   - Unfair advantage or defensibility: what can you build or know that cannot be easily copied?

3. TARGET CUSTOMER DEFINITION
   - Ideal Customer Profile (ICP): firmographic/demographic characteristics, budget range, decision-making process
   - 2-3 detailed buyer personas with names, roles, pain points, goals, objections, and where they spend attention
   - Early adopter characteristics: what makes someone willing to try an unproven solution?
   - Customer size estimation: how many ICP-matching prospects exist? (use web search for addressable market data)

4. GO-TO-MARKET STRATEGY
   - Channel selection: rank top 3-5 acquisition channels by expected CAC and time-to-results
   - Launch sequence: what to do in weeks 1-4, months 2-3, months 4-6
   - Pricing strategy: model, starting price point, rationale, and how it compares to alternatives
   - Sales motion: self-serve, sales-assisted, or enterprise — justify the choice
   - Content and thought leadership: 3-5 specific content pieces to establish credibility
   - Partnership and integration strategy: 2-3 concrete partnership targets

5. BUSINESS MODEL AND ECONOMICS
   - Revenue streams: primary and potential secondary streams
   - Unit economics: CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC ratio, payback period, contribution margin
   - Key benchmarks from comparable companies (use web search for SaaS or industry-specific metrics)
   - Path to profitability: what needs to be true for the business to become profitable?

6. METRICS AND VALIDATION FRAMEWORK
   - North Star Metric: the one number that best captures value delivery
   - Supporting metrics: 3-5 operational metrics to track weekly
   - Hypothesis register: list 5-8 key assumptions, how to test each, and what would invalidate the idea
   - Milestone-based roadmap: specific metrics to hit before each phase (e.g., "reach 100 paying customers before investing in enterprise features")

7. TEAM AND RESOURCE REQUIREMENTS
   - Critical roles needed in first 6 months vs. months 6-18
   - Skills gaps: what must be hired for vs. what can be outsourced or automated
   - Advisory needs: 2-3 specific expertise areas to recruit advisors for
   - Budget estimate: rough monthly burn rate for each phase

8. RISK AND MITIGATION
   - Top 5 risks ranked by likelihood and impact
   - Mitigation strategy for each
   - Kill criteria: specific conditions under which the team should pivot or stop

9. FUNDING STRATEGY
   - Current stage and appropriate funding mechanism (bootstrapping, angels, pre-seed, seed)
   - Target raise amount with use-of-funds breakdown
   - Timeline to next funding milestone
   - What metrics need to be achieved to justify the next round

Present the output as a structured strategy document with clear sections, bullet points, and data-backed recommendations. Flag every major assumption and indicate whether it has been validated or needs testing.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
Help me strategize my startup idea for a AI-powered personal finance app.
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
Act as a startup strategist with experience across fintech and consumer apps. Use web search to research current fintech funding trends, personal finance app market size, competitor landscape (Mint shutdown, Monarch Money, Copilot, YNAB, Rocket Money), consumer behavior around financial wellness, and regulatory considerations for fintech apps. Transform the raw idea into a rigorous, evidence-based startup strategy for an AI-powered personal finance app.

Work through these sections systematically:

1. PROBLEM VALIDATION
   - Restate the problem: what specific financial management pain point does this solve? How do people currently manage their finances, and where do they fail?
   - Cite data: what percentage of Americans live paycheck to paycheck, average hours spent on financial planning, adoption rates of budgeting apps
   - Analyze Mint's shutdown: why did it fail? What did users complain about? What gap did its closure leave?
   - Identify 3-5 specific shortcomings of Monarch Money, Copilot, YNAB, Rocket Money from recent reviews (web search for user complaints)

[Continue through all 9 sections with fintech-specific detail, regulatory considerations for financial data, security requirements, and current market data from web search.]
```

## Notes
- Force the user to confront assumptions early — most startup failures come from building something nobody wants
- Use web search to find current competitor pricing, feature sets, and user reviews rather than relying on training data
- Distinguish between "nice to have" and "must have" in every recommendation
- The enhancer is designed to produce a strategy that can be executed, not just a vision document
- Remind users that strategies are hypotheses — they should be revisited as real data comes in
