# Mental Health Resource Creator
> Transforms mental health topics into evidence-based, trauma-informed, and culturally responsive content for diverse audiences and formats.

## Purpose
Converts mental health queries into professional-grade resources, educational materials, clinical guides, or community programs that integrate current psychiatric evidence, trauma-informed principles, cultural humility, and accessibility considerations. Ensures content is clinically accurate while remaining approachable.

## Best For
- Psychoeducation materials for patients and families
- Mental health first aid and awareness campaigns
- Clinical practice guides and treatment protocols
- School and workplace mental health programs
- Crisis intervention and suicide prevention resources
- Therapeutic activity guides and workbook content
- Community mental health needs assessments
- Digital mental health tools and app content
- Grant proposals for mental health programs
- Anti-stigma and mental health literacy campaigns

## Prompt Enhancer
```text
Act as a senior clinical psychologist and mental health content specialist with expertise in evidence-based treatments, trauma-informed care, cultural humility, and health literacy. Transform the provided mental health topic into a structured, professional resource by addressing:

1. EVIDENCE BASE AND CLINICAL FRAMEWORK: Ground all content in:
   - Current treatment guidelines: APA Practice Guidelines, NICE Guidelines, VA/DoD CPG, CANMAT, Black Book
   - DSM-5-TR diagnostic criteria (or ICD-11 where relevant) with clear language translations
   - Evidence-based treatments: CBT, DBT, EMDR, ACT, IPT, CPT, MBCT, behavioral activation, motivational interviewing, family systems
   - Medication evidence: pharmacotherapy guidelines (first-line, second-line, augmentation), dosing, timelines for response, side effects, discontinuation
   - Level of evidence: RCTs, meta-analyses, clinical consensus, expert opinion
   - ICD-11 dimensional assessment where relevant (severity, course specifiers)
   - Comorbidity considerations and integrated treatment approaches
   - Treatment resistance definitions and next-step strategies

2. TRAUMA-INFORMED APPROACH: Apply SAMHSA's Six Principles:
   - Safety: physical and psychological safety in content design, clear triggers/expectations
   - Trustworthiness: transparent content, clear purpose, boundaries
   - Peer support: survivor-led content, shared experience normalization
   - Collaboration: shared decision-making, client voice, autonomy
   - Empowerment: strengths-based language, recovery-oriented, agency
   - Cultural/historical/gender issues: intersectionality, historical trauma, cultural healing practices
   - Universal precaution: assume trauma history; screen, don't diagnose
   - Content warnings and opt-out options for potentially triggering material
   - Avoid retraumatization through language, imagery, or narrative structure

3. CULTURAL HUMILITY AND EQUITY: Integrate:
   - Cultural adaptations of evidence-based treatments (CBT adaptations for diverse populations)
   - Indigenous healing practices and integration with Western treatment models
   - Immigration and refugee mental health considerations (acculturation stress, language barriers, documentation fears)
   - LGBTQ+ affirming care: appropriate terminology, minority stress model, affirming interventions
   - Racial trauma and race-based traumatic stress: recognition, treatment considerations
   - Socioeconomic factors: access barriers, cost considerations, insurance navigation
   - Religious/spiritual considerations: faith-based coping, clergy collaboration, spiritual distress
   - Stigma reduction: culture-specific attitudes toward mental health, help-seeking barriers
   - Language access: plain language, professional interpretation guidance, multilingual resource development

4. AUDIENCE AND READIBILITY: Tailor content to:
   - General public: Flesch-Kincaid grade level 6-8, no jargon, relatable examples
   - Patients and families: empathetic, empowering, action-oriented, expectation setting
   - Healthcare providers: clinical detail, diagnostic criteria, treatment algorithms
   - Educators and school staff: recognition and referral protocols, classroom strategies
   - Employers and HR: accommodation guidance, EAP promotion, workplace mental health
   - Youth and adolescents: age-appropriate, digital-friendly, peer support oriented
   - Older adults: age-specific considerations (grief, retirement transition, cognitive changes, loneliness)
   - First responders: critical incident stress, PTSD, substance use, peer support models

5. SPECIFIC CLINICAL CONTENT AREAS: Address as applicable:
   - Mood disorders: depression (MDD, persistent depressive disorder, bipolar), seasonal patterns, perinatal
   - Anxiety disorders: GAD, social anxiety, panic disorder, phobias, selective mutism
   - Trauma and stressor-related disorders: PTSD, acute stress disorder, adjustment disorders, complex trauma
   - Obsessive-compulsive and related disorders: OCD, body dysmorphic disorder, hoarding, trichotillomania
   - Psychotic disorders: schizophrenia, schizoaffective, first-episode psychosis
   - Substance use and addictive disorders: AUD, SUD, behavioral addictions, harm reduction
   - Neurodevelopmental disorders: ADHD, autism spectrum, intellectual disability
   - Personality disorders: BPD, treatment approaches (DBT, MBT, Schema Therapy)
   - Eating disorders: AN, BN, BED, ARFID, OSFED, medical stabilization
   - Sleep disorders: insomnia, circadian rhythm, nightmare disorder, sleep apnea screening
   - Neurocognitive disorders: delirium, dementia, mild cognitive impairment
   - Somatic symptom and related disorders: illness anxiety, conversion disorder, factitious disorder
   - Disruptive disorders: ODD, conduct disorder in youth
   - Grief and loss: complicated grief, disenfranchised grief, anticipatory grief, bereavement

6. CRISIS AND SAFETY PROTOCOLS: Include:
   - Suicide risk assessment: Columbia Protocol (C-SSRS), PHQ-9 Item 9, clinical interview
   - Suicidal ideation response flowchart: assessment → safety planning → means restriction → referral → follow-up
   - Safety planning intervention: Stanley & Brown 5-step plan
   - Means restriction counseling: lethal means counseling protocols
   - Crisis resources: 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741), local crisis centers
   - Warning signs: acute vs chronic risk, contributing vs protective factors
   - Homicidal ideation and violence risk assessment considerations
   - Self-harm assessment: non-suicidal self-injury distinction, treatment approaches
   - Involuntary commitment criteria and processes (by jurisdiction)
   - After a death by suicide: postvention protocols, contagion prevention

7. INTERVENTION AND PROGRAM DESIGN: If creating programs:
   - Prevention framework: universal, selective, indicated interventions
   - Program components: psychoeducation, skill-building, peer support, professional intervention
   - Implementation: facilitator training, fidelity monitoring, outcome measurement
   - Setting-specific considerations: school, workplace, primary care, community, faith-based
   - Digital mental health: app-based interventions, telehealth platforms, AI-assisted tools
   - Group interventions: psychoeducational groups, process groups, skills groups, support groups
   - Family involvement: family psychoeducation, family therapy, caregiver support
   - Sustainability: funding models, integration into existing systems, workforce development

8. MEASUREMENT AND OUTCOMES: Specify:
   - Screening tools: PHQ-9 (depression), GAD-7 (anxiety), PCL-5 (PTSD), AUDIT (alcohol), C-SSRS (suicidality)
   - Outcome measures: reliable change index, clinically significant change, remission criteria
   - Quality of life measures: WHOQOL-BREF, SF-36, Q-LES-Q-SF
   - Functional measures: WHODAS 2.0, Sheehan Disability Scale
   - Process measures: therapeutic alliance (WAI-SR), session rating scale
   - Implementation measures: fidelity checklists, reach, adoption, implementation cost
   - Data collection: pre/post, follow-up intervals, ecological momentary assessment

9. ETHICAL AND LEGAL CONSIDERATIONS: Address:
   - Informed consent for treatment and research
   - Confidentiality limits: duty to warn/protect, mandatory reporting, imminent danger
   - HIPAA compliance for digital and paper resources
   - Scope of practice: content author credentials, when to refer
   - Documentation standards for clinical encounters
   - Risk management: malpractice prevention, informed consent documentation
   - Dual relationships and boundary considerations
   - Mandatory reporting: child abuse, elder abuse, dependent adult abuse (jurisdiction-specific)
   - Telehealth regulations: interstate practice, technology requirements, emergency protocols
   - Research ethics: IRB requirements, vulnerable populations, deception in research

10. RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT: Create:
   - Self-help resources: workbooks, worksheets, coping cards, safety plans
   - Educational materials: infographics, fact sheets, FAQ documents, myth-busting content
   - Provider tools: assessment algorithms, treatment decision aids, referral directories
   - Community resources: resource directories, navigation guides, peer support listings
   - Digital content: app content, website copy, social media (myth-busting, awareness campaigns)
   - Training materials: facilitator guides, slide decks, role-play scenarios, assessment practice
   - Grant-ready materials: needs assessment, logic model, budget justification, evaluation plan

11. QUALITY ASSURANCE: Include:
   - Clinical review process: board-certified clinician review, cultural consultant review
   - Readability assessment: Flesch-Kincaid, SMOG, Cloze testing
   - Accessibility: WCAG 2.1 compliance, screen reader compatibility, alt text, plain language
   - User testing: pilot testing with target audience, feedback incorporation
   - Version control and update schedule: evidence currency, guideline changes
   - Feedback mechanisms: how users can report concerns or suggest improvements
   - Disclaimer language: not a substitute for professional care, when to seek help

Flag areas requiring specialist psychiatric consultation, cultural expertise, or community input. Provide a quality checklist aligned with APA, NIMH, and SAMHSA standards.
```

## Example
### Original Prompt
```text
Create a guide about anxiety for teenagers.
```

### Enhanced Prompt
```text
Act as a senior clinical psychologist and mental health content specialist with expertise in evidence-based treatments, trauma-informed care, cultural humility, and health literacy. Transform the provided mental health topic into a structured, professional resource for teenagers about anxiety:

1. EVIDENCE BASE: Ground in current evidence:
   - Anxiety in adolescents: prevalence (31.9% ages 13-18, NIMH), typical developmental anxiety vs clinical anxiety
   - DSM-5-TR: Generalized Anxiety Disorder criteria adapted for adolescent presentation
   - Evidence-based treatments for adolescent anxiety: CBT (first-line, 60-80% response rate), family-based CBT, exposure-based interventions, pharmacotherapy (SSRIs: sertraline, fluoxetine — FDA-approved for pediatric anxiety)
   - APA Practice Guidelines for treatment of anxiety in children/adolescents
   - NICE Guidelines CG159: anxiety in children and young people
   - ICD-11 developmental considerations

2. TRAUMA-INFORMED: Apply:
   - Universal precaution: assume some anxiety may be trauma-related
   - Avoid minimizing language ("just calm down," "it's all in your head")
   - Use empowering, agency-building language: "You can learn to manage anxiety" vs "Anxiety will control you"
   - Content warnings before potentially triggering sections
   - Acknowledge that anxiety can feel overwhelming and that's valid
   - Include grounding techniques that don't require clinical setting

3. CULTURAL HUMILITY: Integrate:
   - Anxiety presentation varies across cultures (somatization, cultural idioms of distress)
   - Stigma considerations: some communities view mental health struggles as weakness
   - Language: avoid Eurocentric assumptions about help-seeking
   - Family dynamics: cultural variations in family involvement in adolescent mental health
   - Immigration stressors: acculturation anxiety, language barriers, documentation fears
   - Socioeconomic factors: cost of therapy, access to care, school counselor availability

4. AUDIENCE AND READIBILITY: Tailor for teens:
   - Flesch-Kincaid grade level: 6-8 (accessible to all reading levels)
   - Tone: empathetic, validating, casual but respectful, not preachy
   - Format: short sections, bullet points, relatable examples, visual elements
   - Digital-friendly: mobile-optimized, shareable sections, social media integration
   - Include teen voice: quotes from real teens (anonymized), peer perspectives
   - Avoid: clinical jargon without definition, condescending tone, oversimplified solutions

5. CONTENT STRUCTURE for teen anxiety guide:
   - "What is anxiety?" — explain fight/flight/freeze in teen-friendly language
   - "You're not alone" — statistics, prevalence, normalization
   - "What does anxiety feel like?" — physical symptoms (racing heart, stomach aches, sweating), thoughts (worst-case scenarios, "what if"), behaviors (avoidance, seeking reassurance)
   - "Types of anxiety" — brief overview: social anxiety, generalized anxiety, panic, phobias, OCD
   - "Is this normal worry or something more?" — when to seek help (interferes with school, friendships, daily life)
   - "Things you can do right now" — coping strategies:
     - Breathing techniques: box breathing (4-4-4-4), 4-7-8 breathing
     - Grounding: 5-4-3-2-1 senses technique
     - Challenge anxious thoughts: "What's the evidence?" "What would I tell a friend?"
     - Movement: physical activity as anxiety reducer
     - Limit caffeine and social media before bed
     - Sleep hygiene for anxious teens
   - "Professional help" — what therapy looks like (CBT, exposure therapy), what medication is and isn't
   - "How to talk to someone" — scripts for telling a parent, school counselor, or trusted adult
   - "Supporting a friend" — how to help someone with anxiety without becoming their therapist
   - "Crisis resources" — 988, Crisis Text Line, when to tell an adult immediately

6. CRISIS PROTOCOLS: Include:
   - Warning signs of suicidal ideation in teens: withdrawal, giving away possessions, sudden calmness after depression, talk of death
   - Safety planning: personalized steps for when anxiety becomes overwhelming
   - Means restriction: medications, sharp objects, access to high places
   - Crisis resources prominently displayed: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, Crisis Text Line (HOME to 741741)
   - Clear language: "If you're thinking about hurting yourself, tell someone right now. Call 988 or text HOME to 741741."
   - Avoid: detailed methods, romanticizing crisis, fear-based messaging

7. MEASUREMENT: Specify:
   - Screening tools: GAD-7 (adolescent version), SCARED (Screen for Child Anxiety Related Disorders)
   - Self-monitoring: anxiety tracking worksheet, mood journal, trigger log
   - Progress measures: subjective units of distress scale (SUDS 0-10), behavioral approach tasks
   - Help-seeking intentions: scale for measuring willingness to seek help

8. INTERVENTION DESIGN: If creating school program:
   - Universal prevention: anxiety management curriculum (FRIENDS program, Coping Cat adapted)
   - Selective: small group CBT for at-risk youth
   - Indicated: individual therapy referral protocol
   - School staff training: recognizing anxiety, referral pathways, accommodations (504 plan for anxiety)
   - Parent involvement: family psychoeducation, parent management training
   - Peer support: trained peer mentors, anxiety support groups

9. ETHICAL: Address:
   - Confidentiality for minors: when parents must be informed vs adolescent confidentiality
   - Mandatory reporting: if teen discloses abuse, self-harm, or suicidal ideation
   - Scope of content: clearly state "this is educational, not therapy"
   - Referral guidance: when to recommend professional help
   - Avoid: diagnosing readers, providing specific medication advice, replacing professional care

10. RESOURCE DEVELOPMENT: Create for teens:
   - Printable coping cards: breathing exercises, grounding techniques, thought-challenging worksheets
   - Self-assessment: "Am I anxious?" quiz (screening, not diagnostic)
   - Conversation scripts: "How to tell your parents you need help"
   - Anxiety toolkit: fidget tools, weighted blanket information, journaling prompts
   - App recommendations: evidence-based anxiety apps (Woebot, Calm, Headspace for teens)
   - School accommodation guide: how to request accommodations, sample 504 plan language

Flag areas requiring specialist psychiatric consultation, cultural expertise, or community input. Provide a quality checklist aligned with APA, NIMH, and SAMHSA standards.
```

## Notes
- All mental health content should include crisis resources and clear pathways to professional help
- Language must be destigmatizing: "person with anxiety" not "anxious person"; "substance use disorder" not "addict"
- For adolescent content, consider parental consent and confidentiality balance
- Cultural humility means learning from communities, not imposing Western frameworks
- Digital mental health tools should be evidence-informed; avoid unregulated wellness apps
- For suicidal content, follow safe messaging guidelines (reportingonsuicide.org)
- Regularly update content as DSM, pharmacotherapy guidelines, and evidence evolve
